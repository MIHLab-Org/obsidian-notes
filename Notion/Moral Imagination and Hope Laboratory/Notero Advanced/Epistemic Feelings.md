Abstract: This chapter focuses on some feelings that enter into the epistemic processes of inquiry, knowledge and metacognition. Epistemic feelings seem to serve precisely the function of providing premises elaborated at the subpersonal or intuitive level for use in explicit inferences. The chapter looks at how fear affects belief and concentrates some examples of what refers to as epistemic feelings. It describes some surprising recent findings about the influence of oxytocin on trust and about the direct influence of social conformity on perception and belief. Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) used to be thought of as a neurotic syndrome calling for psychoanalytic diagnosis and therapy. Remarkably some of these cases are capable of clearing up under the influence of a drug such as Prozac. A crucial epistemic feeling can be triggered or at least facilitated by a simple chemical agent, which apparently determines the presence or absence of conviction in a particular proposition.
