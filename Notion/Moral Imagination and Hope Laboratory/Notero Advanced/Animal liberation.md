In his original *Animal Liberation, *Peter Singer might be thought to have suggested that what preserves African American dignity is different from what preserves human dignity more generally. I think that this is a common finding, but one that I have struggled with because it seems to me that the traditional struggle was to defend humanity against a more broad society that wanted to deny that African Americans deserve the attribution of dignity in all the same ways that other Americans, i.e., European Americans do. I think that a good motivation for this critique can be found in the opening chapter of Peter Singer’s “Animal Liberation”. 

He begins by recounting a claim about women’s rights in 1792 that argued that the defense of the rights of women is similar to the defense of the rights of “brutes”, namely that any such defense would be absurd. That “If the argument for equality was sound when applied to women, why should it not be applied to dogs, cats, and horses?” Singer of course disputes this claim, but what he needs to do is argue that equality for women, a human person, is grounded differently than equality for animals, a non-human person. This is what he fails to do however. 

He begins first by arguing that equality does not imply that we need to treat groups of people on the basis of race, or sex differently because how we treat them depends on their natures. One example that he uses is based on sex, arguing that it would be absurd to demand the right to abortion for men (any human regardless of accepted pronoun usage, for instance a human who identifies as a woman but is incapable of becoming pregnant, that is incapable of becoming pregnant) since men (humans who are incapable of becoming pregnant) are incapable of becoming pregnant. 

Because of this, he expresses an attempt to defend a claim of equality that does not need to deny differences between human beings. But the mistake occurs when he argues that the kinds of differences that are relevant here are those on which discrimination is grounded in whether related to race or sex. It is one thing to accept differences between human beings who are incapable of becoming pregnant and those who are not incapable of becoming pregnant. But it is not clear what similar differences are in terms of race. 

> we will see that we would be on shaky ground if we were to demand equality for blacks, women, and other groups of oppressed humans while denying equal consideration to nonhumans.
