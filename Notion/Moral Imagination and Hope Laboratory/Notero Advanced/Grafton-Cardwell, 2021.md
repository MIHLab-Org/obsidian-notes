Abstract: I introduce and explicate a new functionalist account of art, namely that something is an artwork iff the fulfillment of its function by a subject requires that the subject aesthetically engage it. This is the Aesthetic Engagement Theory of art. I show how the Aesthetic Engagement Theory outperforms salient rival theories in terms of extensional adequacy, non-arbitrariness, and ability to account for the distinctive value of art. I also give an account of what it is to aesthetically engage a work that relies on our agential capacity to treat an object as having non-instrumental value, even while the ultimate purpose for our engaging the object is to get something from it.

# Aesthetic Engagement Theory of Art

## Aesthetic Engagement Theory of Art

Traditional Concept of Aesthetic Functionalism:

Something is an artwork iff the fulfillment of its function by a subject requires that the subject aesthetically engage it.

- Provides us with aesthetic experiences
	- meant to do so
	- normative that it does so
- What provides us with aesthetic experiences?
	- something is an artwork because of how it is in itself, not how it’s regarded
	- what makes this is what it is for, which is its function
	- this function is an artwork’s aesthetic dimension
- Other kinds of functionalist, remember Functionalist say artworks are for something.:
	- Proceduralist deny 1 and 2.
	- Representationalist say something is art iff represents or has content about something.
	- Expressionist: something is art iff it expresses emotion.
	- Formalist: something is art iff it has significant form (or was intended to have significant form).

## Aesthetic Engagement Theory

- What makes something an artwork is the fact that one can’t interact with it the way it’s meant ot be interacted with unless one aesthetically engages it.

“An object can have some function F not in virtue of a maker’s intention that the object have F, but in virtue of the fact that the maker has made something of a certain kind K.15” (Grafton-Cardwell, 2021, p. 256)

### Fulfillment Requirement

“For an object’s function to be fulfilled by a subject S is for S to interact with the object in the way it’s for” (Grafton-Cardwell, 2021, p. 257)

What aesthetic objects are for is that they are interacted with as a source of instrumental value, so that they are interacted with for no other reason than to be interacted with.

“To fulfill the function of an artwork often requires that we treat the artwork as a source of instrumental value (e.g., as a source of knowledge, or as a meditative focus) even while simultaneously subjecting ourselves to it as if it were an object of final value.” (Grafton-Cardwell, 2021, p. 266)
