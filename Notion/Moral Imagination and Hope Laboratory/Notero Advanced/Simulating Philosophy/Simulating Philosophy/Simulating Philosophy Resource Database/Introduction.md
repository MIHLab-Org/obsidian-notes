Two approaches to video game criticism:

- Ludic approach: Games are a unique media best understand by focusing on modes of interactivity that distinguish them [from one another?] [[[Unit Operations An Approach to Videogame Criticism]]; [[Persuasive games the expressive power of videogames]]; [[Gamer theory]]; [[Half-real]] 
- Narrative Approach: Video games are like other forms of media and should be judged in terms of plot, character development, and narrative. [[More than a game]] [[Hamlet on the holodeck]] 

Instead argues that games can be “interpreted as virtual thought experiments” to explore counterfactuals.
	- They can be interpreted as modeling philosophical problems
	- Support or challenge theories

- Therefore they can “improve on traditional narrative thought experiments”
	- They include an execution element
	- allow the experiment to actually be performed by situating them within simulated worlds that are far more complex and detailed than the settings described in most narrative thought experiments.

Thought experiments can be used to assess myriad theoretical and empirical research questions [[Thought Experiments in Science and Philosophy]] 

- Game narratives set out explicit problems that raise philosophical questions. 
- Gameplay mechanics set the rules that govern players’ range of choices. 
- Interpreting games as thought experiments also goes beyond the narratological and ludological positions. 
	- The function of thought experiments cannot be fully captured by narratological analysis, as games are not simply texts to be read and interpreted; they encourage audiences to give answers to the questions they pose.
