Games are a unique social technology, a method for inscribing forms of agency into artifactual  vessels: for recording them, preserving them, and passing them around.

1. Games are a form or artistic agency and therefore in order to exercise this unique artistic agency, we must pursue the goals of the game wholeheartedly. In other words, we must engage the goals of the game aesthetically, an obligation of the game designer. The game designer does this by creating specialized rules of the game which in turn enable us to take on artificial forms of agency.

Ultimately then, games have a motivational structure.

2. Games require us to strive towards achievment.
3. They do this by submerging us, or immersing us, enabling us to take on alternate forms of agency.
4. By doing so, games play an active role in the development of our own agency. [?]
5. These are aesthetic demands [taking on alternate forms of agency, striving towards achievement, developing our own agency].
6. This aesthetic agency is socially maintained by prescribing attention.
7. Because they are socially distanced in this way [socially], the aesthetic qualities of games are distanced from the designer and the game itself.
8. Therefore games have important social and moral consequences of agential manipulations of the game.
9. So games also work in the medium of sociality.
10. Therefore it is possible for games to threaten our autonomy.
11. Aesthetic striving offers some protections against this threat.
