## Introduction

Prior Claim:
    Philosophy in the Anglo-American tradition prizes lucidity, analysis, careful distinction, and rigorous argument.
        characterized by attention to analytic detail, predilection for precision

Criticism:
    Therefore Anglo-American philosophy its focus is narrow, it lacks breadth
        but this attention to detail leaves out a lot of what is philosophically interesting about the world.
        e.g., what is central to interpersonal relations
            [[Brain circuits and functions of the mind  essays in honor of Roger W. Sperry]] (The role of the right cerebral hemisphere in evaluating configurations)
            [[Cerebral lateralization]] 
        This approach also makes it inaccessible to most people
> [analytic] metaphysicians interpret what we initially understand into something hardly anyone understands, and then insist that we cannot do without that. ([[The Empirical Stance]], 3)

Sub-Conclusion:
> Therefore, left to itself, because it values intricate, technically expert argument, the analytic approach has a tendency to focus more and more on less and less; and so, at its worst, it can become plodding, pedestrian, sterile, and inadequate to its task ([[Notion/Moral Imagination and Hope Laboratory/Notero Advanced/Wandering in Darkness/Wandering in Darkness]], 24).

Conclusion
    Given the short-comings of analytic philosophy, we would be better off in becoming experts of personal relations by studying works by creators of literature, storytellers.
    Therefore we should use narratives to study, become experts in, personal relations.

## Analytic Philosophy and Narrative

Claim:
    First, at this best, analytic philosophy can be very good at large and important problems.
    But left to itself, the analytic approach has a tendency to focus more and more on less and less, and so, at its worst, it can become plodding, pedestrian, sterile, and inadequate to its task.
        When analytic philosophers need to think about human interactions, they tend not to turn to complex cases from real life or from great literature
        In other words, it often neglects the “messy and complicated issues involved in relations among persons.
        To argue that analytic philosophy’s primary problem is its narrowness, is imprecise. Rather analytic philosophy can only see one half of the problem
> It is therefore misleadingly imprecise, I think, to diagnose the weakness of analytic philosophy as its narrowness. Its cognitive *hemianopia* is its problem. Its intellectual vision is occluded or obscured for the right half of the cognitive field
    We should use narratives and analytic philosophy to study problems related to personal relations, such as the problem of evil.
