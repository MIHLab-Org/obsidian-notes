> If the mind, in particular, was to be respected either scientifically or even as meaningfully describable in the first place, mental ascriptions would have to be pegged to publicly, physically testable verification-conditions. [[Notion/Moral Imagination and Hope Laboratory/Notero Advanced/Mind and Cognition/Mind and Cognition]] p. 3

The phenomenon of pain. for example, is determined by “wincing-and-groaning” behavior.

Some mental states are inner and episodic. So not identified with behavior or dispositions to behave. Alos not dualist since episodic mental items are not ghostly or nonphysical. 

They are neurophysiological, identical with states and events occurring in their owners’ central nervous systems.

Every mental state or even is numerically identical with some such neurophysiological state or event

Identity theory and “types” or “kinds” of mental items.
    e.g., a mental state such as pain has always and everywhere the neurophysiological characterization initially assigned to it.
> However, this constraint on the biology of any being “capable of feeling pai is both gratuitous and indefensible, why should we suppose that any organism must be made of the same chemical materials as we in order to ahve what can be accurately recognized as pain?

        As such, the identity theorists overresponded to behaviorists and committed species chauvinism.
    Fodor and Putnam respond: 
> What was important was not its being c-fibers taht were fireing, but what the c-fibers were doing, what their firing contgributed to the operation of the organism as a whole.
> Thus, to be in pain is not per se to have c-fibers that are firing, but merely to be in some state or other, of whatever biochemical description, that plays the same causal role as did the firings of c-fibers in the human beings we have investigated.
