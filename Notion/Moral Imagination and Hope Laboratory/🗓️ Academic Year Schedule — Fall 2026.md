# 📚 The Core Principle: One Activity

> [!note] 📌
> Reading, writing lecture notes, and working on publications are the same activity.
> When you read a source:
> 
> - Your reading note in Notero IS your lecture prep for the course that covers this material
> - It IS your literature review entry for the article that engages this source
> - It IS the raw material for your publication
> 
> You do not read three times. You read once and link the note to the course AND the publication.
> 
> The 7:00–8:30 AM Deep Work block every day is when this happens. Everything else — grading, email, admin, prep — happens after. Never before.

# 📊 Workload Reality Check

You are teaching **4 sections = 16 credit hours of instruction**. This is a heavy load for a research-active faculty member. The national norm for research-active faculty is 2–3 courses. Here is what that means practically:

- **Grading:** With 3 Intro sections, you likely have 60–90 students. Weekly reflections = 60–90 papers per week. Budget **3–4 hours/week for grading** during heavy periods.
- **Prep:** Because your reading notes ARE your lecture notes, prep time should be minimal once the system is running. Target: **30–45 min per course per meeting** for review, not new prep.
- **Research:** Protect the morning Deep Work block. 1.5 hours × 5 days = **7.5 hours/week of research time** even during the semester. That is enough to maintain momentum on one article.
- **Job market:** Budget **3–4 hours/week** during September–November specifically for applications.

> [!warning] ⚠️
> **The risk:** With 4 courses, the job market, and a young family, the schedule above is tight. The single most important protective move is keeping the 7–8:30 AM Deep Work block inviolable. If you let teaching prep colonize that block, research stops and the job market suffers. Do not let it happen.
