## Part 1 — About the Moral Imagination and Hope Laboratory

The **Moral Imagination and Hope Laboratory (MIHL)** is a research initiative directed by Montaque Reynolds, PhD, at Stetson University in DeLand, Florida. MIHL investigates how imaginative and virtual environments — narrative, aesthetic, and interactive — support moral development, wellbeing, and the conditions for human flourishing.

The lab’s core claim is that certain kinds of environments are not merely entertaining or recreational. They are spaces where genuine philosophical work happens — where questions about consciousness, identity, presence, vulnerability, and the good life can be not just discussed but inhabited.

**Current MIHL projects:**

- *Playing at the REAL Good Life* — a weekly philosophical reading group pairing primary texts with in-game thought experiments (Discord + Twitch, Saturday evenings)
- MIHL Experiments — structured in-game experiments testing philosophical claims about consciousness, moral emotions, and virtual experience
- *Philosophers in the Verse* — this interview series

**Philosophical grounding:** Chalmers, Stump, Marcel, Nussbaum, De Cruz, Zhai, Gualeni

**Platforms:** Twitch (live), YouTube (archived), Substack (written essays)

## Part 2 — Why Interview Inside Star Citizen?

The standard podcast interview format — two people on Zoom, talking *about* ideas — has a structural limitation: the conversation is always about something happening somewhere else.

Star Citizen allows us to think *inside* a virtual world rather than describe it from outside. This changes the conversation in three ways:

**1. The environment responds.** Star Citizen is a live, persistent universe. Other players are present. Missions have genuine consequences. Ships can be damaged or destroyed. These are not illustrations of philosophical points — they are the conditions under which philosophical questions naturally arise.

**2. The setting is the argument.** If we are discussing virtue and moral formation, we might run a cooperative mission where the choice of how to treat a wingmate under pressure surfaces exactly the questions your work addresses. If we are discussing wonder and awe, we fly toward a nebula. The location is chosen to make the philosophical question feel, not just sound, real.

**3. It is genuinely novel.** No existing podcast or interview series conducts philosophical interviews inside a persistent-universe game. The format is itself a philosophical experiment.

You are welcome to be skeptical. Many of the best moments in our sessions have come from guests who found the format strange and said so.

## Part 4 — Option B: Hybrid Format (Alternative)

If Star Citizen is not feasible — due to system requirements, time constraints, or preference — there is a fully supported alternative.

**How it works:** Before our session, I record a series of short in-game video clips (5–15 minutes each) capturing the locations, missions, or moments most relevant to your work. The interview takes place over Zoom. During the conversation, we share my screen and watch the clips together — pausing, reacting, and discussing what we see.

This format preserves the core purpose of the series — using the game environment as a philosophical prompt — while removing the technical requirements of live in-game participation.

**What you need for the hybrid format:**

- A working microphone
- A Zoom account (free tier is fine)
- 90–120 minutes of uninterrupted time

**Clip selection:** I will send you a brief description of the clips I plan to record, tied to the themes of your work. You are welcome to suggest locations, scenarios, or mechanics you would like to see.

## Part 5 — What to Expect on the Day

**Session structure (~90–120 minutes):**

| Phase | Duration | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival and orientation | 15–20 min | Informal arrival in-game or first clip together. Unstructured conversation — what do you notice? |
| The interview | 60–80 min | Main conversation on your work, the philosophical questions it raises, and what the environment around us reveals. |
| Debrief | 15–20 min | What did the game surface that a conventional interview wouldn’t have? |

**Recording and streaming:**

- Session will be recorded
- With your permission, streamed live on Twitch and archived on YouTube
- A written essay accompanies each session on Substack — you will receive a link to review before publication
- Recording-only (no live stream) is available on request

**Audio tips:**

- A USB condenser mic (Blue Yeti, AT2020) is ideal
- A headset mic is acceptable
- Built-in laptop mic is a last resort — use headphones to avoid echo
- Find a quiet space if possible

## Part 6 — Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to be a gamer or know Star Citizen?**

No. I handle all navigation and technical gameplay. Your only job is to think and talk. If you find the environment strange or disorienting, say so — that reaction is philosophically interesting.

**Will I look ridiculous crashing a ship on camera?**

Possibly. It has happened before. The crash is data.

**How much time do I need to prepare?**

Beyond reading this document and our pre-session call, no preparation is required. You know your work.

**Can I see the questions in advance?**

I prefer not to send a fixed question list — the best conversations are responsive to what happens in the game. I can share broad themes and the in-game scenario. If you want more structure, let me know.

**Is there an honorarium?**

Not at this stage — MIHL is a self-funded research initiative. What I can offer is a well-produced video and essay that present your work to a new audience. If grant funding becomes available, I will revisit this.

**Who is the audience?**

Primarily: gamers who think seriously about the philosophical questions games raise, and philosophy readers interested in virtual worlds. Secondary: veterans, students, people interested in consciousness and wellbeing.
