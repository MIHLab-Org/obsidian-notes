## 🎮 The Concept

Welcome to “The Philosophers in the Verse”, a series of interviews conducted by us at the Moral Imagination and Hope Laboratory. Each week, we host a different philosopher who talk to us about different philosophical issues related to both physical and narrative thought experiments found in philosophical treatises. Each interview takes place inside Star Citizen where the guest and host fly, walk, and explore the game world together while the conversation about virtual worlds unfolds. 

A conversation about virtual identity happens while the guest’s ship is in danger of destruction. A conversation about community and moral formation happens while navigating Port Olisar or Area 18. A conversation about wonder and awe happens while flying toward a nebula.

This week we will be talking with Nicholas Baima, an associate professor of philosophy at Florida Atlantic University and with Sarah Malanowski, the co-author of the book “Why its okay to be a Gamer”. In why its okay to be a gamer, Baima and Malanowski argue Gamers should play games with redeeming aesthetic/intellectual qualities that justify the use of of their features and elements (like violence).

1. Gamers should play games with redeeming aesthetic/intellectual qualities that justify the use of of their features and elements (like violence).
2. Games can be used to develop both virtue and vice.
3. One way that games can develop virtue is by cultivating friendships.
4. However, games can be addictive and it is up to developers to remove these elements from their games.
5. Given 1-3, and if we implement 4, then gaming is not a waste of time since it can develop virtues like friendship, tolerance, appropriateness, chillness, fortitude, honesty, humility, kindness, and moderation.

The format inverts the usual podcast: instead of two people in chairs talking *about* games, two philosophers are *inside* one, thinking out loud.

**What makes this distinctive:**

- No existing podcast or interview format conducts philosophical interviews inside a persistent-universe game
- The in-game environment responds to the conversation in real time (missions, events, other players)
- The guest’s own philosophical work becomes a lens for interpreting what happens during the session
- Every interview is a genuine MIHL experiment — filed in the Experiments Plan database

## 🌟 Priority Rationale

**Start with Malanowski and/or Baima** — they are at Florida Atlantic University, which is in Florida. They work explicitly on virtue theory and games. Their new Routledge volume is timely. They are accessible junior/mid-career scholars rather than famous gatekeepers. A Florida philosopher-to-Florida philosopher introduction is natural, not presumptuous.

**C. Thi Nguyen** is the most prominent figure in philosophy of games right now — his APA Book Prize makes him a marquee name. His work on agency as the art form of games is directly relevant. He is also known to be publicly engaged and accessible.

**Stefano Gualeni** is the single closest academic to what MIHL does — he literally uses virtual worlds as philosophical tools and has written about existential reflections on virtual worlds. An interview in Star Citizen with Gualeni would be extraordinary.

**Helen De Cruz (post humous, tribute of authors)** is already in your reading group curriculum and your Notero. She knows the territory and her work on wonder and awe maps directly to your sessions.

## 🎮 In-Game Interview Design Notes

**The format has three movements:**

6. **Arrival and orientation (15–20 min)** — Guest arrives in-game and gets oriented. You fly together to a starting location chosen to match the interview’s themes. Conversation is informal — what do you notice? What does this feel like?
7. **The philosophical conversation (60–80 min)** — The main interview, conducted while flying, walking, or running a structured mission. The mission is chosen to surface the guest’s philosophical questions. You might run a cooperative mission with Malanowski to discuss virtue and community. You might fly toward a nebula with De Cruz to discuss wonder and awe.
8. **The debrief (15–20 min)** — What did the game surface that the studio wouldn’t have? What did the guest notice that they didn’t expect?

**Location suggestions by guest:**

| Guest | Suggested location/mission | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malanowski / Baima | Cooperative bounty hunt or cargo run in Stanton | Virtue as practiced disposition — tested by actual cooperative play |
| C. Thi Nguyen | Custom racing or exploration mission | Agency as art — the game as a system that shapes the contours of possible action |
| Gualeni | Exploration — uninhabited planet or deep space | Existential dimensions of virtual worlds — thrownness, finitude, care |
| De Cruz | Exploration — nebula or gas giant approach | Wonder and awe — the emotion of being confronted with something larger than yourself |
| Silcox | High-risk mission with permadeath framing | Defense of simulated experience — does genuine risk make it more real? |
