**Module: The Pilot's Extended Mind
Demonstrate that the ship’s HUD and flight systems are not just "tools," but active constituents of the pilot's cognitive process.**

**Phase 1: The Internalist Baseline (Manual Labor)
In this phase, the student must perform a task without "epistemic aid."**

Goal: Travel to a location on a planet without flight aids.
To do - destination must be a landable planet that is:
- easily identifiable / visible from the starting location
- has a large and unmistakable feature like a signature crater
- is tidally locked so our destination isn't accidentally in darkness during the exercise

Task Overview: Ensure the ship's Supercruise Assist and Advanced Docking Computer modules are disabled before departure.  The student must take off, visually identify the destination, direct the ship toward it and enter supercruise.  Speed and heading are to be adjusted manually.  Proceed to the specified location on the planet's surface (touching down inside a large crater).
To do - Regarding HUD data that appears automatically during descent:
- in accompanying step-by-step instructions, direct students to AVOID using the HUD indicators during final approach
- focus should be kept on the visual information beyond the cockpit glass
- optional follow up question: Since it was visually present, did you find it difficult to avoid using the HUD anyway?  What difference does it make?


Reflection Question: "When you were estimating your deceleration to avoid overshooting the destination, or landing safely, where was the 'calculation' happening? Was it purely in your mind, or was the ship helping you?"

Notes:
Internalist limitations: By forcing the use of only visual cues and gut reactions during their approach, this can impart a sense of "high cognitive load" and demonstrate the limits of the biological brain under complex and unusual circumstances (relative to daily life for most people).

"Glide Slope" trigger: When the glide indicators appear, they are an example of an Epistemic Action provided by the ship. They don't move the ship; they change the display to provide additional information.  If these are successfully ignored and glide fails (due to an overly steep descent angle), we can point out that internal cognitive capacity was insufficient for the task, which is why we "extend" our minds into the ship's computer and instrument displays.

**Phase 2: The Coupled Pilot (Extended Mind)**

Objective: To demonstrate how external symbols (HUD elements) drive cognitive processes in real-time, creating a "Coupled System" where the ship and pilot function as a single unit.

Scenario: The Precision Instrument Landing
Instead of a giant, unmistakable crater, the student is tasked with finding a station at a set of Planetary Coordinates (Latitude/Longitude).
To do - Making this more interesting:
- there are many situations we could substitute for this scenario that rely on HUD information, the current setup is an example
- 1st step would be listing these
- likely need to rewrite the current example to address the mixed bag of navigational aids and manual flight presented by approaching a ground station

Task Overview: The student must navigate to a specific point using the HUD's coordinate display and the heading compass.  Land at the station at the coordinates.

During this flight, the student isn't looking at the ground to "feel" their way down. They are performing Epistemic Actions:

Coordinate Matching: They are altering the ship's pitch and yaw specifically to make the numbers on the HUD change. They aren't "flying to a place"; they are "solving a numerical puzzle" that results in being at a place.

The Landing Hologram: Once they are over the spot and low enough, the holographic landing grid appears. This is another Epistemic Action that transforms complex 3D spatial alignment into a simpler task of aligning the blue circles.

Lab Instructions (Phase 2)"Engaging the Coupled System"

Objective: Locate and land at the provided planetary coordinates (Lat/Long).

Procedure: Do not look for visual landmarks. Instead, focus entirely on the HUD Coordinate Display (located above your fuel gauge) and your Heading Compass (the degrees at the top of your HUD).

```plain text
- Notice how your hands operate the controls based on the numbers. You aren't 'calculating' your position; the ship is presenting your position in a way that maps directly to your motor skills.
- When the landing hologram appears, use it to center your ship. Observe how the hologram allows you to 'see' your ship's alignment from an angle your physical eyes can't.
```

Reflection Questions

The Boundry Question: "When you were adjusting your heading to match the coordinates, were you 'thinking' about the math of a sphere, or were you simply reacting to the HUD? If you were reacting to the HUD, wasn't the ship's computer doing the 'navigational thinking' for you?"

The Parity Principle: "If a future neural implant gave you these coordinates directly in your 'mind's eye' (Case 3 in the Tetris example), would that feel any different from looking at the HUD? If the result and the process are the same, is there any reason to say the HUD isn't part of your mind?"

Epistemic vs. Pragmatic: "When you pitched the nose down to see the landing zone better, was that a Pragmatic Action (moving the ship to get closer) or an Epistemic Action (moving the ship to get better information)?"

**Phase 3: The "Broken Mind" (The Contra-Positive)
This scenario presents a moment where the student experiences the loss of behavioral competence when the external component is removed.**

Goal: Engage in a low-stakes combat scenario (perhaps at a Nav Beacon in a High Security system).

The Task:

1. Start the fight normally using the Targeting Reticle (The Extended Mind in action).
2. Once the enemy's shields are down, the student must disable their HUD display (CTRL+ALT+G) while continuing to fight.

The Philosophical Result: Without the HUD, the pilot still knows how to fly and shoot (Internalism), but they can no longer "think" effectively about the target's distance, lead, or health. Their "behavioral competence" has dropped because their Extended Mind was severed.

Reflection Question: "When the HUD was gone, did you feel like you became 'less intelligent' as a pilot? If your competence dropped, does that prove the HUD was part of your mind?"

Summary -
Internalism:  Visual navigation.  The ship is a passive vehicle.
Externalism:  HUD assisted flight. The ship is a cognitive partner.
The Contra-Positive:  HUD is turned off.  The "Mind" is broken; competence fails.

"Debrief" Questions for the Student
The Skin/Skull Boundary: If the neural link (Case 3 in your Tetris example) felt exactly like using the HUD (Case 2), why do we tend to think the neural link is "inside" and the HUD is "outside"?

Coupled Systems: If you spent 1,000 hours flying this ship, does the COVAS (ship voice) become a part of your mind's capacity for memory/remembering?  Or is it just a recording that triggers at certain times to describe events?

Pragmatic vs. Epistemic: When you deploy your landing gear, are you changing the world (Pragmatic) or changing your information (Epistemic)? What about when you switch on Night Vision mode, when flying through a completely dark area?
