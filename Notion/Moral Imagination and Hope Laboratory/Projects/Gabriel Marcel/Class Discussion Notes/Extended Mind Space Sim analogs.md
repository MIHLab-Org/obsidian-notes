Elite Dangerous

Basic:

Epistemic actions... we have plenty of actively engaged elements beyond basics like temp and fuel gauges, although even the simple stuff could still be considered epistemic:

- spatial display of sensor contacts nearby (the central radar circle)
- Directional indicator for the currently selected destination or hyperspace jump
- leading/lag weapon pips to visually calculate optimal firing position for each hardpoint, based on projectile speed per weapon type 
- speed indicator bar with “blue zone” showing the range of optimal maneuverability; display changes depending on current flight mode and gravitational conditions
- lines showing the orbits and gravitational exclusion zones of celestial bodies, used to safely navigate in supercruise and employ gravity assisted acceleration/braking
- Power distributor indicator bars showing remaining power for ship’s systems, engines, and weapons; pips beneath each show allocation of powerplant capacity
- Fuel gauge 

Advanced:

We can get more into Active Externalism with things like:

- Supercruise Assist. Automatically maintains course and speed, drops from supercruise on arrival
- Advanced Docking Computer. 3 functions: Auto Docking, Auto Launch, and Auto Landing (differentiated from Docking since surface landings also use the computer to locate a suitable landing site)
- Galaxy Map. Plots a route between 2 points consisting of jumps between star systems, using selectable parameters to prioritize either speed or fuel efficiency. Organizes many possible information types per the pilot's discretion on a visually display.
- Flight Assist. With FA ON: the ship performs the cognitive work of providing counter-thrust to keep the ship's heading and vector aligned (ie, flying in the same direction it faces). FA ON quickly cancels out yaw/pitch/roll inputs from the pilot to keep from spinning continuously on each axis. This facilitates a simplified control scheme akin to low-speed aircraft and is intuitively easier to manage. If FA is turned OFF: the pilot becomes responsible for applying all* counter-thrust to maintain control of the ship's vector, AND to stabilize its momentum along 6DOF (six mechanical degrees of freedom). Unless the pilot consciously acts, the ship's heading becomes decoupled from its vector, often intentionally to perform complex maneuvers.
