## I am a philosopher and educator who is deeply passionate about leveraging storytelling to enhance human reasoning, productivity, ethical decision-making, and overall flourishing—particularly for underserved youth. 

In 2024, I obtained a PhD in Philosophy from Saint Louis University under the care and tutelage of Helen De Cruz who has since passed at a much too young age. It is only now that I am beginning to recognize how much Helen shaped me to be the philosopher that I am and now, following her example, and with a small team, I build and integrate AI-driven tools—such as D&D campaign generators and develop massively multiplayer online game environments—into narrative driven educational platforms which foster critical thinking, moral imagination, and empathy. As Founder and Director of the Moral Imagination and Hope Lab, formerly known as the Saleno Center for Human Flourishing, I lead initiatives that explore how narratives and games can counter harmful stereotypes, promote moral well-being, build community, and guide users toward more thoughtful, connected lives. I am currently serving as Visiting Assistant Professor of Philosophy at Stetson University, where I teach courses in logic, aesthetics, philosophy of law, and introduction to philosophy, philosophy of m. I am currently developing courses in the philosophy of mind and personhood to be taught in the 2026-2027 academic year. In my work and teaching, I always emphasize real-world applications, collaboration, and rigorous ethical reflection. My work, including forthcoming publications like Moral Autonomy and Personhood in Pop Culture, draws on narrative ethics to show how fiction and interactive media can cultivate affective moral understanding and support a more examined, meaningful existence.

I use a model of instruction taught to me by my mentor of over 10 years to determine what objectives I need to focus on and how to improve my classroom instruction. I assign a weekly short writing assignment with a stringent set of requirements for successful completion. In one paragraph, the students have to identity and express the main thesis of the writing, two to three premises that support that thesis, and two to three supporting statements of those premises. They can use three sentences to express the thesis, premises, and supporting statements. The last two sentences of the paragraph explains their response and why, either because of the veridicality of the author's argument, or veracity of one of the author's claims. This tells me what the students are understanding from the course material, as well as what they are getting from our discussions. I always see incredible improvement over the course of the semester using this method.

While I do not hold a valid teaching certification, I do have extensive experience in using pedagogical models to instruct a wide array of learners. I have taken a number of seminars and workshops designed to improve my curriculum, instruction, assessment, and pedagogy. While at Saint Louis University, I attended several workshops in the Reinert Center for Transformative Teaching and Learning. I am also a fellow of the Saint Louis Digital Humanities Network, a group of post-secondary, secondary, and primary educators dedicated to improving education in the Saint Louis region.
