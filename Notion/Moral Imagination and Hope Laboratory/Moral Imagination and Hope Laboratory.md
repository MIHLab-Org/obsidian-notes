> [!note] 👋
> **MIHLab** is a new way to explore philosophy by actually *experiencing* it rather than just studying definitions.
> Most philosophy education works one way: a teacher explains a concept like "justice" by breaking it down into principles and definitions. But there's another approach that's often more powerful—learning through real examples. You understand justice better by encountering a genuinely just person, or reading their story, than you do by memorizing a definition.
> 
> MIHLab brings this second approach to life. We create immersive virtual worlds where you can step inside philosophical questions and explore them hands-on. Instead of asking "What is justice?" in a lecture hall, you might find yourself navigating a complex scenario where you have to decide what the just choice is, or meeting characters who embody different ways of thinking about a problem.
> 
> The idea is simple: some of the most important questions in human life—about justice, beauty, courage, meaning—aren't best learned from textbooks. They're best learned by exploring them directly, with a guide, in a world where the consequences feel real.
> 
> - **Art’s capacity to ignite ethical reflection** on our deepest bonds—family, friendship, love—and the meaningful paths toward union with God.
> - Interactive Ethics How video games create moral dilemmas that force players to confront their values in ways traditional philosophy cannot.
> - Music & Moral Identity How artists use narrative to help listeners process emotions and imagine ethical commitments.
> - Aesthetic Sentimentalism Bridging aesthetics and ethics to understand how beauty, emotion, and moral knowledge intertwine in popular art forms.
