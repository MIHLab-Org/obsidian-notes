I am excited to submit my application to Pacific Lutheran University for the position of Community Directed. As a committed Christian educator with a PhD in Philosophy and extensive experience teaching critical thinking, logic, and ethical reasoning, I am deeply inspired by PLU’s mission—to educate students for lives of thoughtful inquiry, service, leadership and care of and for others, their communities and the planet earth. My background in epistemology, moral philosophy, and AI-driven ethical modeling positions me to help guide students to steward knowledge responsibly, from a position of care and compassion. I am a philosopher and educator who is deeply passionate about leveraging storytelling to enhance human reasoning, productivity, ethical decision-making, and overall flourishing—particularly for under-served populations. In 2024, I obtained a PhD in Philosophy from Saint Louis University under the care and tutelage of Helen De Cruz who has since passed at a much too young age. It is only now that I am beginning to recognize how much Helen shaped me to be the philosopher that I am and now, following her example, and with a small team, I build and integrate AI-driven tools—such as D&D campaign generators and develop massively multiplayer online game environments—into narrative driven educational platforms which foster critical thinking, moral imagination, and empathy. 

As Founder and Director of the Moral Imagination and Hope Lab. I lead initiatives that bridge narrative and philosophical thinking in order to counter harmful stereotypes, promote moral well-being, build community, and guide users toward more thoughtful, connected lives. I am currently serving as a visiting Assistant Professor of Philosophy at Stetson University, where I teach courses in logic, aesthetics, philosophy of law, introduction to philosophy, philosophy of mind. In my work and teaching, I always emphasize real-world applications, collaboration, and rigorous ethical reflection. My work, including forthcoming publications like Moral Autonomy and Person-hood in Pop Culture, draws on narrative ethics to show how fiction and interactive media can cultivate affect and moral understanding and support a more examined, meaningful existence.

In my current role as Visiting Assistant Professor of Philosophy at Stetson University I emphasize collaborative reasoning, evidence-based inquiry, and real-world applications—skills foundational to scientific method and lab work as these are important for critical self-reflection also. Previously, as Adjunct Philosophy Instructor at Lewis & Clark Community College, I designed Contemporary Moral Problems (Ethics) courses that integrated AI ethics, data interpretation, and collaborative

problem-solving, helping students analyze complex systems and moral implications. Through MIHLab, I develop AI-driven tools

and interactive programs (e.g., ethical scenario generators) to advance moral well-being and

critical thinking among under-served youth—which are directly transferable to inspiring stewardship of ourselves, the earth and places that we inhabit, and the communities that we reside in. My dissertation on the moral and spiritual dimensions of sentiment in fiction further honed my ability to connect empirical observation with deeper questions of human dignity and purpose.

I am eager to contribute to Pacific Lutheran University as a lecturer of religion. I am passionate about lifelong learning through professional development and I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my interdisciplinary expertise can support your students. Thank you for considering my application.
