# Multiday Adventure Curriculum Design (draft)

Back to [[eduLARP Resource Drive]] · Sources: [[eduLARP Resources Report]] · [[Lumen Script Book Notes]]

> **Status:** this is a starting proposal for Monty to revise, not a finished curriculum. Its structure borrows from *Lumen* (EDGE), its world from Bible 5e, and its trip logistics from The Quest booklet. No published program runs eduLARP on a moving, multiday expedition, so the adaptation is new and untested.

## 1. Design principles
1. **The route is the story.** Each day's trail segment is one chapter, and the hardest climb carries the story's crisis (Quest booklet §22).
2. **Borrow Lumen's skeleton, not its theology.** Keep its goal setting, ritual, mid-game and end debriefs, and safety calibration. Leave out deity embodiment and the therapeutic framing. *Lumen* is **CC BY-NC**, so MIHLab writes its own scripts in its own words and doesn't copy theirs into a paid program.
3. **Scripture as the broad script.** Each day pairs a passage that *argues with itself* or leaves a question open. The game never tells a player what they are "for."
4. **Open to everyone.** No prior belief is needed to play. Passages are offered as part of the story and the conversation, never as a test.
5. **Non-contact play.** Conflict is resolved with dice at the campfire, following Scouting's no-striking rule.
6. **Parents play too.** Every family plays as a linked pair or small party.
7. **Teach before you play.** *Lumen* found that teaching lessons inside the game confused players, so the learning content goes in a pre-trip workshop and the trail is for play.

## 2. Default shape: three days, two nights
The six *Lumen* lessons fold into three days.

| Day | *Lumen* lessons | Story beat | Trail | Passage (KJV) | Evening |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (night before or morning) | 1 · Goal setting | Seekers are called | Trailhead | Jeremiah 1:5; 1 Samuel 16:7 | Session zero: characters, safety tools, learning goals, then the **countdown and welcome crossing** into the story |
| 1 | 2 · Ritual · 3 · Identity | Crossing the threshold | Easy first segment | Joshua 4 (memorial stones); 2 Corinthians 5:17 | Mid-game debrief |
| 2 | 4 · Conflict · 5 · Inclusion | The crisis | Hardest climb | Genesis 33 (Jacob meets Esau); Ruth 1; Acts 10 | Campfire resolution with dice |
| 3 | 6 · Culture · close | Return home changed | Way out | Acts 17:22–28 | **Claiming ritual** ("I claim ___") with the blessing scene (Quest booklet §42.1); countdown out; full debrief |

**Five-day summer variant:** give each *Lumen* lesson its own day, and add a rest day after the crisis for skills and a service project.

## 3. Daily rhythm
| Time | Block | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | **Crossing ritual** in a semicircle ("I am ___ and today I'm seeking ___"), countdown in, OK check-in | *Lumen* welcome ritual |
| Trail | Play in character; roles are real trip jobs (Pathfinder, Healer, Keeper of Lore, Quartermaster) | Quest booklet §23 |
| Rest stops | Encounters, puzzles and dilemmas from the dilemma bank | Quest booklet §24 |
| Campfire | Dice resolution, token moves, the passage read aloud, **closing crossing and countdown out**, then a debrief circle (≤12 people; de-role line; "I" statements; no crosstalk) | *Lumen* closing ritual and debrief script |
| Bedtime | Journey journal entry | Quest booklet §23 |

## 4. Character-axis tokens (Monty's mechanic)
- **Four quadrants:** lawful good, unlawful good, lawful evil, unlawful evil.
- **Start:** 5 tokens in each quadrant, 20 in all. Even spread is the neutral starting point.
- **Play:** the DM moves a token from one quadrant to another after a significant choice.
- **Over time:** the tokens gather, showing what kind of person the *character* is becoming.
- **Ties to existing pieces:** the virtue tokens (Quest booklet §23) and *Joshua 4*: the stones are memorials of what was done.

⚠ **Design flags to decide:**
1. **Character, not child.** MIHLab's guardrail is that feedback describes acts, never fixed verdicts on a person. Say "your *character* moved toward unlawful good when they freed the prisoner," never "you're evil." Reset or retire characters between adventures.
2. **"Evil" quadrants with young players.** Keep them, since they make choices meaningful, but decide whether the DM announces moves publicly or tells each family privately.
3. **The theology.** The axis tracks *character*, which MIHLab leaves open to be wrestled with. It doesn't measure *worth*, which Scripture settles. Saying this out loud at session zero protects the frame.

## 5. Story world options
| Option | Source | Strength | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-century journey | *Adventurer's Guide to the Bible* | Ready lineages, NPCs and an adventure for levels 1–10 | 5e combat focus; check its handling of Scripture |
| Original premise (Lamplighters, Cartographers' Guild, Pilgrims of the Spring) | Quest booklet §21 | Fully MIHLab's own; easy to fit to a Florida trail | Must be built from scratch |
| Hybrid | Both | Biblical world, MIHLab's mechanics | More prep |

## 6. Staffing and safety
- **Guides:** *Lumen*'s NPC Guides become trained volunteers who play roles and watch the group. *Lumen* uses **one Guide per group of 4**, so plan on one Guide per family or per two small families. Guides hold space and never push their own plot. The first trips use an outfitter (Quest booklet §58).
- **Safety tools:** session zero, X-Cards and Veils set in advance, "Off-game:" calls, OK check-in, Lookdown, a pause signal (Quest booklet §25), and *Lumen*'s safety and calibration workshop.
- **The wilderness lead overrides the story,** always.

## 8. What reading *Lumen* changed
- Added crossing rituals and countdowns in and out of the story each day.
- Debriefs now happen every night, using *Lumen*'s circle rules.
- Lessons move to a pre-trip workshop.
- Guide ratio set at 1:4.
- **Archetype cards ↔ tokens:** *Lumen* gives each player two cards, *who I've been* and *who I want to become*. That could pair with the token board: the cards name the hoped-for direction, and the tokens show what the character's choices actually did.
- **Two ideas worth testing as dilemma mechanics:** Lesson 3's *Inner Committee* (players voice a character's Inner Parent, Inner Child and Inner Critic at a hard choice) and Lesson 4's *conflict styles* (replay the same argument in a different style).
