## 1. What it is
- An **eduLARP**: players learn transformative role-play design *by playing*. Inside the fiction, characters design and run short "nano-games" for each other.
- **Story:** strangers ("Seekers") arrive at a retreat called **Hope**, where ancient deities and magic help them change. Guides (NPCs) hold the space.
- **No dice or GM.** You play with your whole body, in real time. Metatechniques signal powers and safety.
- **Explicitly not therapy:** "The Guides are not in a client-therapist relationship with the participants." Mental-health professionals are recommended as Assistant Guides or on the safety team.
- **Not for complete beginners.** It recommends having some experienced larpers among organizers and players.
- **Modular:** many sections run as standalone exercises.
- **Values statement:** the authors describe the change they encourage as "prosocial and progressive in nature," aimed at "greater peace and justice in the world for all people."

## 2. Scale and staffing
| Item | *Lumen*'s guidance |
|---|---|
| Length | 3–4 days: 2 workshop days, about 1.5 days of play, debrief on day 4 |
| First run | 42 players and Guides, at a convent outside Florence |
| Groups | **"Gemstone" groups of 4** (no larger), each with **1 Guide** (1:4) |
| Pairs | Each player has a **supportive pair** (designs for each other) and a **challenging pair** (works through tension) |
| Safety | Assistant Guides double as the in-fiction safety team; off-game safety team during all waking hours |
| Site | Minimal. "Any country house, scout group house, or accommodation for group holidays"; 2–3 common rooms; some outdoor space |
| Props | Archetype and Fate card decks (about 2 per 36 players), badge holders, optional satin sashes, simple robes |
| Tech (optional) | Unity-built Lumen AR app (Android/iOS); VRChat world "Convento dell'Incontro" |

## 3. The three-phase model (ritual structure)
From van Gennep and Turner, as used throughout EDGE:
1. **Before / preparation:** workshops, lectures, costuming, goal setting.
2. **During / liminal:** the game itself.
3. **After / return:** de-roling, debriefing, integration.

**Three kinds of goals**, kept distinct because each needs different care: *transformative impact* (leisure), *therapeutic goal* (therapy) and **learning objective (education)**. Goal categories: educational, emotional processing, social cohesion, political aims.
→ **For The Quest:** use *learning objectives* language only.

## 4. Core schedule (Ch. 7)
**Day 1, pre-game:** briefing and goals workshop · X-Cards and Veils · 1-hr lecture · How to Play · Metatechniques · Safety (1 hr) · **Lessons 1–6 (45 min each)**
**Day 2, pre-game:** character creation and relations (2.5 hrs) · calibration · costuming
**Day 2, game:** Welcome ritual · Gemstone groups · goals workshop · deity embodiment · Closing ritual · **mid-game debrief (1 hr)**
**Day 3, game:** challenging pairs · scenario design task · **4 playtests of 45 min each** · Encountering Challenge ritual · Claiming ritual
**Day 4:** end-of-game debrief (1 hr+)

**Essential modules if short on time:** Welcome ritual · goals workshop for the scenario task · scenario design task and worksheet · playtests · Claiming ritual.
**Untested modules (written after the run):** Challenging Pairs workshop · Encountering Challenge ritual · Lesson 7: Embodiment 101.

**Lesson from their run:** running the Lessons *in-game* didn't work well, since players couldn't tell whether to critique as themselves or as characters. They now recommend teaching all Lessons **before** the game.

## 5. The six Lessons (Appendix F)
Each lasts 45 minutes and includes its own workshop and debrief.
| # | Lesson | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Transformative leisure design | Leisure vs. education vs. therapy goals |
| 2 | Ritual | Preparation, liminal and return phases; three myth patterns (creation, hero's journey, maturation); practice a life-journey ritual |
| 3 | Identity (intrapersonal) | Players enact an **"Inner Committee"**: Inner Parent, Inner Child, Inner Critic, Memories of the Past |
| 4 | Conflict (interpersonal) | Play a conflict in one **Thomas-Kilmann style** (competing, accommodating, compromising, avoiding, collaborating), then replay it in another; from Erasmus+ ROCKET |
| 5 | Inclusion (positional) | "Odd person out" scene; groupthink and conformity |
| 6 | Culture design (ideological) | Co-create a ritual for a fictional culture's belief, then play it briefly |

## 6. Rituals worth borrowing
- **En-role countdown:** a Guide counts down from 10 to 1 over a semicircle of players ("10…9…8 You have just arrived at Hope…").
- **Welcome ritual:** players cross from one side of a semicircle to the other, stating "I am ___ and my intention is ___." Their Guide hands them a badge: "Welcome to Hope. I will be your Guide."
- **Archetype cards:** each player picks one card for *the journey so far* and one for *who they want to become*, both carried in the badge holder. Day 1 shows the first; Day 2 flips to the second.
- **Closing ritual:** cross back the other way, followed by a 10-to-1 countdown out of the fiction.
- **Claiming ritual (finale):** "I am ___. I claim ___," then each player makes the **Choice** (stay at Hope or go out into the world) by which side they cross to.

## 7. Safety tools (Ch. 3, Appendix E)
- Guiding principle: **"The only person who gets to decide what happens to your character is you."** Activities are opt-*out*.
- **X-Cards and Veils** are set before play. Their run X-carded child abuse, bullying, sexual abuse, animal cruelty, domestic violence and present threat of suicide. Players add a second round after character creation, and no one is asked why.
- **"Off-game:"** a prefix for anything out of character ("Off-game: softer," "Off-game: be right back," "Off-game: opt-out").
- **OK check-in:** an O-hand signal answered by thumbs up, so-so or thumbs down.
- **Lookdown:** a hand over the eyes means "don't interact with me."
- Ask consent before backstory scenes that touch X-carded topics.

## 8. Debrief script (Appendix L)
- Debrief **every day**: short versions mid-game, the full version at the end with integration options. About 60 minutes, opt-out.
- Sit in a circle of **no more than 12**, with the facilitator in it.
- **De-role line:** "Hello. My name is [player]. In this larp, I played [character]. Right now I need [___]."
- Rules: "I" statements · **no crosstalk** · 3–5 minutes each per question · passing is allowed, and the facilitator returns to anyone who passed · no "war stories" · no design feedback during the debrief.
- The facilitator holds space and doesn't comment on each share.
- After the game, **integration practices**: creative expression, intellectual analysis, emotional processing, interpersonal processing, community building, returning to daily life.

## 9. Guides (Ch. 5)
- Guides hold **power**, whether they feel it or not. Use it to help Seekers, never to push the Guide's own plot.
- They **hold space** and don't generate content. Any task a Guide suggests must serve the Seeker's own goal; a goodbye ritual is the example given.
- Qualities: mature, emotionally intelligent, good listeners, able to set boundaries and hand off to the safety team.
