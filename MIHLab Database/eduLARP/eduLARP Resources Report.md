# eduLARP Resources Report

Back to [[eduLARP Resource Drive]]

**Short answer:** for curriculum, the richest free source is the Erasmus+ **EDGE** project. It's a complete, openly licensed curriculum on designing role-play for personal change, with two ready-to-run larps (*Lumen* and *Meet Me Halfway*) whose workshop, safety and debrief scripts are written out in full. **Bible 5e** supplies the biblical setting and rules, but it's a tabletop D&D setting, not an eduLARP method. The two fit together: EDGE gives the *structure* of a learning game, and Bible 5e gives the *world*.

*Sources were read on 2026-11-02 from the project pages and search results. Items marked ⚠ couldn't be verified; DriveThruRPG blocked access.*

### 1.1 Curriculum: five courses
Each is 10 ECTS at half time (about 20 hours a week). The courses were originally designed by Sarah Lynne Bowman, Josefin Westborg, Elektra Diakolambrianou and Josephine Baird, and some include material from the related Erasmus+ ROCKET project. [Announcement](https://erasmusedge.eu/full-edge-curriculum-resources-now-available/)

### 1.2 The all-materials bundle
One zip on Zenodo: https://zenodo.org/records/22896664 ([announcement](https://erasmusedge.eu/erasmus-edge-all-materials-bundle-now-available-for-download/)). It contains:

- the five course OERs above, plus 6 curriculum lectures and 4 modules on technology integration
- **Textbook:** *Transformative Role-playing Game Design* (eds. Bowman, Diakolambrianou, Brind)
- **Textbook:** *Implementing Transformative Role-playing Games* (eds. Bowman, Hugaas, Brind)
- **EDGE Tech Toolkit v2.0** (Taisto Suominen), in accessible and graphical versions
- **Meet Me Halfway** larp script (Bowman, Rydberg, Bandhoesingh), in accessible and graphic versions
- **Lumen** script book plus a folder of additional materials (Section 2)

### 1.3 Course 1 in hand: *Introduction to Transformative Game Design*
✅ In vault: [[Introduction to Transformative Game Design OER.pdf]] (80 pp.; Bowman, Westborg, Diakolambrianou, Baird, Suominen; **CC BY-NC 4.0**)
- A 10-week asynchronous Master's course: discussions, reflection journals, two video-conference playtests and two major assignments (a design proposal, then a full transformative game design with peer review).
- **Units:** 1 Introduction · 2 Transformative leisure RPGs · 3 Therapeutic RPGs · 4 **Educational RPGs** · 5 Research through design · 6 **Ritual, myth and symbolism** · 7 Culture and conflict · 8 **Safety and identity** · 9 Game technologies · 10 Transformative game design and you.
- **For MIHLab:** Units 4, 6 and 8 are the most relevant to The Quest. Units 5 and 9 bear on the research arm and the games studio.

### 1.4 Most useful for MIHLab
1. **The *Implementing* textbook**, for running role-play safely with real groups.
2. **The *Lumen* workshop scripts**, which are a ready template for pre-game, mid-game and end-of-game sessions.
3. **The Bachelor's course**, the most approachable entry to the design method.

## 2. *Lumen: The Journey to Hope* (larp script book)

| | |
|---|---|
| Authors | Simon Brind, Sarah Lynne Bowman, Kjell Hedgard Hugaas, Josephine Rydberg, Alessandro Giovannucci, Taisto Suominen |
| Published | 19 Aug 2026 (v1; a newer version exists) |
| DOI / license | 10.5281/zenodo.22012066 · book says **CC BY-NC 4.0** (Zenodo listing says CC BY 4.0) |
| File | ✅ In vault: [[Lumen Script Book -- Erasmus EDGE Consortium.pdf]] (196 pp.) · **Full reading notes: [[Lumen Script Book Notes]]** |
| Print | Print-on-demand on DriveThruRPG: https://tinyurl.com/5h9rn4hm ([announcement](https://erasmusedge.eu/print-on-demand-of-lumen-script-book-now-available/)) |
| Players, length, ages | 3–4 days; first run had 42 players and Guides; groups of 4 with 1 Guide each; not recommended for complete beginners; adult audience |

**Premise:** strangers ("seekers") attend a retreat at a mysterious place called Hope, where magic is said to come to those who need it. Through unusual rituals they face personal struggles and help one another through change. Players set personal goals in pre-game workshops, and NPC **Guides** facilitate. Inside the fiction, characters learn transformative game design and make small "nano-games" for one another.

### 2.1 The six-lesson arc
This is the most reusable part for a multiday design.

| # | Lesson | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Transformative leisure, educational and therapeutic goal setting | What do I want to grow in? |
| 2 | Ritual | Marking thresholds |
| 3 | Intrapersonal | Identity |
| 4 | Interpersonal | Conflict |
| 5 | Positional | Inclusion |
| 6 | Ideological | Culture |

### 2.2 Ready-made scripts in the materials folder
- **Workshops:** welcome and goal setting, how to play this larp, character creation, meta-techniques, safety and further calibration, the challenging pair, deity embodiment, transformative goal
- **Ritual script:** Encountering Challenge
- **Debrief scripts:** mid-game and end of larp
- **Cards and props:** archetype cards, fate cards, deity cheat sheets, badges (guide, organizer, safety, safety guide, seeker), and a scenario-creation worksheet

⚠ **Fit check for MIHLab:** *Lumen* includes deity embodiment and mystical-practice rituals, and its frame is partly therapeutic. Borrow the **structure** (goal setting, ritual, mid-game and end debriefs, safety calibration), not the religious content. Keep The Quest framed as education and formation, not therapy (see the Quest booklet, §32).

| File (already in vault) | What it is |
|---|---|
| [[Bible-Player-Guide.pdf]] | *Adventurer's Guide to the Bible*: player guide and character creation |
| [[SRGtS-SailorGuide.pdf]] | *Silk Road Guide to Seafaring*: sailor guide and character creation |
| [[AGttA-PlayerGuide.pdf]] | *Azael's Guide to the Apocalypse*: player guide and character creation |

**How to use it:** take the world, the lineages and the NPCs, and run them as **non-contact** live role play, resolving combat with dice at the campfire. That matters because Scouting's *Guide to Safe Scouting* bans games where players strike each other. Older precedent: Green Ronin's *Testament* (3rd edition) covered the same ground.

⚠ **Read before using with families:** 5e assumes combat and includes demons and angels as monsters. Check how the book treats biblical figures and miracles against MIHLab's guardrail of saying "Scripture teaches" accurately, and decide what's appropriate for young players. I haven't read the book itself.
