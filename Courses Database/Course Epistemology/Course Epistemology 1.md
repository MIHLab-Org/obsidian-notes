## About this project

<!-- Column 1 -->
This course explores the question of concept of knowledge, or of knowing. Epistemology is the study of knowledge more broadly. It includes reflections on such questions as “what does it mean for one to hold a justified belief”? Is belief in God warranted? What counts as evidence for belief? Whether knowledge is more foundational than mere belief and so on. It is structured in 9 parts, which include: skepticism which asks whether we can know that there is an external world, and whether we can know anything at all; the structure of knowledge and justification, e.g. empirical knowledge and justification; we will also look to properly define knowledge and at the various attempts that have been made to do so; epistemic closure which attempts to look at and define what we can know by implication; the various theories of justification; epistemic virtues, warrant, cognitive responsibility which asks whether we have a responsibility to obtain knowledge in the right way. We will also look at what it means for a person to be a source of knowledge, otherwise known as naturalized epistemology. Among other questions that are central to knowing.
