# Do We Know?

## The External World

What do we know about the external world? If we have doubts, then we would be a kind of skeptic. There are two forms of skepticism, Skepticism vs. Cartesian Skepticism. It will be important to try to understand their differences. A skeptic is someone who casts doubt on our beliefs. But there are two kinds of skeptic, the one who casts doubt on all our beliefs, and one who only casts doubts on our beliefs in a certain domain. Consider the difference between “Nobody knows nothin’” and “I don’t believe nothin’ the lyin’ media says”. The first kind of skeptic is a global skeptic while the second is a local skeptic. For instance, we might think that I have “no reason to believe nothin’ the lyin’ media says” because “the lyin media don’t know nothin’”. Here, I am only casting doubt about what the media knows, and by extension anything that I know assuming that the media is my source of knowledge.

## Our Senses

Epistemologists, philosophers who focus primarily on questions of knowledge, largely accept that there are roughly six sources of knowledge. These include:
	- Modal rationalism, which is a kind of reason from universals to particulars. If all the objects in a box are round, then any object that I take from that box will be round.
	- Our senses, these include perceptions such as sight, hearing, etc.
	- But philosophers also look at more interesting cases such as knowledge from “intuition” and ask whether this counts, or
	- knowledge from imagination, we largely think that if I can know how another person feels, then this is largely because of my faculty of imagination. Of course we are often, though not always, wrong!

Descartes asks that if our dreams can deceive us, then how do we know that anything counts as an actual source of knowledge since we could be dreaming?

## Dreams and Other Illusions

Descartes makes a distinction between dreams and illusions however. According to this distinction, dreams are not exactly illusory.
	- Illusion Argument: Our senses have deceived us before. How can we know they aren’t deceiving us now?
	- But dreams can be more convincing, dream argument: Dreams are like reality. How do we know we’re not dreaming?
