# Course Video Production Guide

Based on *Reality+* by David Chalmers · 24-Video Series · Summer 2026

This guide contains three parts: the **Video #1 intro script**, the **full 24-video series schema**, and a **DaVinci Resolve (Free) workflow reference**.

# Part 1 — Video #1 Script: Course Introduction

> 🎬 **Production Note:** Record this narration as voiceover while your avatar moves through a visually interesting area of Star Citizen — a space station, a ship interior, or ideally in transit between planets. The script is written to sync with that kind of ambient movement footage.

**Tone:** Conversational & casual · **Target length:** 5–8 min · **Narrator:** In-game avatar

---

## Opening — Hook (approx. 45 seconds)

*[AVATAR walking through a space station or flying in a ship — wide establishing shot]*

"So... where are you right now?"

"You might say: I'm at my desk. Or on my phone. Maybe in bed. But here's a question that philosophers have been asking for literally thousands of years — and that we're going to spend this whole course wrestling with:"

"How do you actually know that?"

"How do you know the world you're perceiving right now is the real one? How do you know you're not in a simulation?"

*[CUT TO: close-up of avatar face or hands interacting with something in-game]*

"I'm going to go out on a limb and guess you haven't spent a lot of time worrying about that. Most people don't. But once you start pulling on that thread... it gets very weird, very fast."

---

## Introducing the Course (approx. 1 minute)

*[AVATAR panning shot — show the in-game world around you]*

"Welcome to Adventures in Technophilosophy. I'm your instructor, and — as you can see — I'm teaching this course from inside a video game."

"That's not just for fun. Well, it is a little bit for fun. But it's also the whole point. This course uses virtual worlds — specifically this one — as a kind of living laboratory for philosophy."

"The book we're working through is called *Reality Plus* by philosopher David Chalmers. And Chalmers makes a genuinely radical argument: that virtual worlds like this one aren't fake versions of reality. They ARE reality. Different, sure. But real."

"That's a big claim. We're going to spend the course figuring out if he's right."

---

## What Is Philosophy? (approx. 1.5 minutes)

*[AVATAR standing still — more reflective moment]*

"Now, before we go any further, I should say something about philosophy itself, because intro philosophy courses have a reputation. They ask a lot of questions and give very few answers. That's... accurate. But it's not a bug. It's the whole point."

"The word philosophy literally means love of wisdom. Not possession of wisdom — love of it. The pursuit. Philosophers are basically the kids who never stopped asking why."

"Why is there something rather than nothing? What does it mean to know something? What makes an action right or wrong? What even IS reality?"

"Ask those questions a few times in a row and you rapidly hit the foundations — the assumptions so deep that most people never even notice they're there. Philosophy is the discipline that drags those assumptions into the light."

"In other disciplines — science, history, math — you start with those foundations already in place and build up from them. Philosophy is the one that asks: but wait, should we accept those foundations at all?"

---

## The Simulation Hypothesis — Ancient & Modern (approx. 1.5 minutes)

*[CUT TO: in-game footage — something that looks distinctly artificial or dreamlike]*

"The central question of this course — could we be in a simulation? — sounds like it comes from The Matrix. And it does, kind of. But the idea itself is ancient."

"A Chinese philosopher named Zhuangzi, writing around 300 BCE, described dreaming he was a butterfly — and then waking up, and wondering: was he a man who'd dreamed of being a butterfly, or a butterfly now dreaming of being a man?"

"In India, the ancient text of the Yoga Vasistha tells the story of Narada, a sage who falls into a kind of waking dream — an entire lifetime, complete with family and heartbreak and death — only to wake up and realize none of it was real."

"And of course there's Plato's Cave — prisoners chained underground, watching shadows on a wall, convinced those shadows ARE reality."

"Same question, every time: How do you know? How do you know the world you're experiencing is the real one?"

"The only thing that's changed since Zhuangzi is that now we have a specific, technological version of the question: what if it's not a dream, but a computer?"

---

## Course Structure & Objectives (approx. 1.5 minutes)

*[AVATAR moving again — maybe boarding a ship or walking to a destination]*

"So here's how this course is organized. We follow Chalmers' book through seven parts:"

"Part One is about Virtual Worlds — what they are, whether they're real, what the simulation hypothesis actually claims."

"Part Two is about Knowledge — epistemology. Can we know anything? Can we prove there's an external world at all?"

"Part Three tackles Reality itself — metaphysics. What IS real? Is the universe made of information? Could God be a programmer?"

"Part Four zooms in on Virtual Reality specifically — are VR headsets illusion machines, or do they give us access to a different kind of real?"

"Part Five is about Mind — consciousness, the mind-body problem, whether digital beings can be aware."

"Part Six is about Value — can you live a good life in a virtual world? Do simulated lives matter morally?"

"And Part Seven goes deep into the foundations — language, mathematics, and some of the strangest ideas in all of philosophy."

"This is a summer course, so we're moving fast — one part per week, roughly one video per chapter. Each video is short. Each one has one main idea. And each one uses something from this game as a thought experiment."

---

## Closing (approx. 45 seconds)

*[AVATAR — final shot, looking out at space or a horizon]*

"I want to be honest with you: this course won't give you a lot of tidy answers. That's not what philosophy does. What it does is give you better questions — more precise, more honest questions about things you thought you already understood."

"And hopefully, by the end, you'll look at virtual worlds — and maybe reality itself — a little differently."

"Alright. Let's start with the most basic question there is: Is this the real life?"

*[END CARD or fade out]*

## Step-by-Step Production Workflow

### Step 1 — Import Your Footage

1. Open DaVinci Resolve and create a new Project (e.g. "TechnoPhilosophy Ep01").
2. Go to the **Media Pool** (bottom-left on the Edit page). Right-click → **Import Media**.
3. Import your in-game footage AND your recorded voiceover audio file.
4. Right-click your footage → **Scene Cut Detection** — DaVinci will automatically find every cut in a long recording and split it into individual clips.

### Step 2 — Build Your Timeline

5. Drag your **voiceover audio** to the timeline first — it becomes your backbone.
6. Listen through and mark moments where you reference specific in-game visuals (press **M** to drop markers).
7. Drag your in-game footage clips onto the video track, aligning them with your narration markers.
8. Use the **Blade tool (B)** to trim clips. Use the **Selection tool (A)** to move them.

### Step 3 — Clean Up Your Audio (Fairlight Tab)

Click the **Fairlight** tab at the bottom of the screen.

**Voice Isolation** — removes background noise:

9. Select your voiceover clip on the timeline.
10. Open Effects panel → find **Voice Isolation** under Fairlight FX.
11. Drag onto your clip. Use the slider to control strength.

**Dialogue Leveler** — evens out your volume automatically:

12. Select your voiceover clip.
13. In Effects, find **Dialogue Leveler** under Fairlight FX.
14. Set target loudness to **–14 LUFS** (standard for online video).

**Auto Ducking** — lowers background music when you speak:

15. Place in-game ambient audio or music on a separate track below voiceover.
16. Right-click the music track → **Auto Duck**.
17. Set duck amount (–12 to –18 dB) and attack/release times.

### Step 4 — Add Captions (Free Workaround)

18. Export your finished audio as WAV: **Deliver tab** → uncheck Export Video → select **Wave** format → export.
19. Go to [whisper.ai](http://whisper.ai/) or any free Whisper tool to transcribe the WAV file.
20. Download the resulting **.SRT** file.
21. Back in DaVinci: **File → Import → Subtitles** → select your .SRT file.
22. Captions appear as a subtitle track. Style them in the **Inspector panel**.

> 💡 **Tip:** YouTube's auto-captions are also reasonably accurate and free after upload — a valid alternative if you're pressed for time.

### Step 5 — Color Grade (Color Tab)

23. Grade one clip you're happy with, then right-click the viewer → **Grab Still** to save it.
24. Apply that grade to all other clips using **Apply Grade** — keeps your series visually consistent.
25. For in-game footage, a slight contrast boost and subtle warm tone reads well on screen.

### Step 6 — Smart Reframe (If Needed)

26. Right-click a clip on the timeline → **Smart Reframe**.
27. Choose target aspect ratio (9:16 for vertical, 1:1 for square).
28. DaVinci automatically tracks the subject and keeps them in frame.

### Step 7 — Export

29. Click the **Deliver** tab.
30. Choose **YouTube** preset or **H.264 Master** for a high-quality local file.
31. Set resolution to **1920×1080** (1080p).
32. Click **Add to Render Queue** → **Render All**.
