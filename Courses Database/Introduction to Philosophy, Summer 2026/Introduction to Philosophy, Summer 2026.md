## About this project

<!-- Column 1 -->
This course uses the simulation hypothesis to explore one perennial question in philosophy, simulating meaning and value. I use the term perennial here because many of the questions in philosophy are enduring, enduring, recurring, long lasting, etc. The simulation hypothesis is one of these questions as we will see and so combined with the question of meaning, this becomes a very tall order. Although it is famously depicted in the Matrix movies, where what seems to be an ordinary physical world turns out to be the result of connecting human brains to a giant bank of computers, the concept itself extends back thousands of years, albeit without computers of course. How can we trust what our senses tell us, how do we know where to find meaning and value? Philosophy translates as love of wisdom, one of the most frustrating aspects in a philosophy course, is that we ask questions rather than provide answers. The questions we’re asking are especially hard. There are other disciplines for the easy ones. Philosophers are like little kids, we ask annoying questions that should have easy answers. What is love? or How do you know when you’re in it? or What is meaningful? Should meaning matter? or Why shouldn’t I ask that? Ask those questions a few times in a row and you rapidly reach people’s patience. This is because you’re examining the assumptions that underlie things they take for granted.

The objectives of this project are:
	1.  First, I want to use technology to address some of the oldest questions in philosophy, especially the problem of the external world.
	2.  We’ll also use technology to illuminate traditional questions about the mind and value: How do mind and body interact in a way that is meaningful?
	3.  We also use technology to illuminate traditional questions about value and ethics, why isn’t virtual murder wrong but other virtual acts are?
	4. Along the way, we will also use technology to ask other questions such as:
		1. Is there a God?
		2. What is the universe made of? 
		3. How does language describe reality?
		4. What does science tell us about reality?
