    ### Possible worlds and thought experiments
> Experimental philosophy is a new movement that uses systematic experimental studies to shed light on philosophical issues. In other words, experimental philosophers apply the methods commonly associated with psychology (experimentation, statistical analysis, developmental research, reaction time studies, patient studies, and so on), but they use those methods to address the kinds of questions that have been traditionally associated with philosophy. ([https://philpapers.org/browse/experimental-philosophy](https://philpapers.org/browse/experimental-philosophy))

> As a result, simulations are not just a guide to our actual universe. They are also a guide to the vast cosmos of possible universes. Philosophers call these possible worlds.

	- Philosophers generally use the term ‘thought experiment’ fairly loosely to refer to hypothetical scenarios that are used to challenge or substantiate a theory.
		- First, thought experiments can be used to challenge a theory by identifying a difficult case that it cannot answer.
		- Second, thought experiments can model situations that may be untestable using empirical methods.
		- Finally, thought experiments are much less dangerous than real-world experiments, both in the senses of their risk of physical harm and their moral harm.
	- Traditional relies on the imagination
	- Emerging methodologies are more rigorous
	- Emerging methodologies are also more expensive

### Coupled Cognition & Two Forms of Externalism

If I had a twin on a different planet, we could both come to the same belief about water and *twin* water (on the distant planet), that it is wet.

- human organism linked with an external entity
	- components in the system play an *active* causal role that jointly governs behavior
	- removing external component reduces behavioral competence
	- therefore coupled process counts equally well as a cognitive process
- Putnam (1975) and Burge (1979) Externalism:
	- passivity: external features (only internal ones) “play no role in driving the cognitive process in the here-and-now.”
	- removing the external component does not reduce behavioral competence.
	- therefore coupled process does not count equally well as a cognitive process.

---

### Active Externalism

Coupled cognition $\rightarrow$ environment can have a direct impact on the organism and its behavior.

Therefore:

- Either: external features in the environment play a role in cognitive processes
- Or: external features in the environment do not play a role in cognitive processes.

---

### The Contra-Positive

What are the implications for either view?

- If the external features of the environment **do** play a role in cognitive process $\rightarrow$ removing them **[will]** reduce behavioral competence.
- If the external features of the environment **do not** play a role in the cognitive process $\rightarrow$ removing them **[will not]** reduce behavioral competence.
