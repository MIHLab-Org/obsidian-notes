## 🎯 Big Questions This Week

- How do we come to know things?
- What are the different sources and levels of human knowledge?
- How does a medieval thinker’s epistemology compare to Chalmers’s?

---

## 💡 Bonaventure’s Four Kinds of Light

Bonaventure argues that human knowledge comes through four distinct kinds of “light” — each illuminating a different domain of reality.

| Light | What it Illuminates | Domain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. External Light** | Mechanical skill and the arts | Practical crafts |
| **2. Lower Light** | Sense perception | Natural forms |
| **3. Inner Light** | Philosophical knowledge | Reason |
| **4. Higher Light** | Grace and Sacred Scripture | Spiritual truth |

---

## 🔨 1. External Light — Mechanical Arts

Enlightens the mind for appreciation of arts and crafts:

1. Weaving
2. Armor-making
3. Agriculture
4. Hunting
5. Navigation
6. Medicine
7. Fine arts

**Two purposes of the mechanical arts:**

8. **Consolation** — fine arts (pleasure, beauty)
9. **Comfort** — everything else (practical utility)

---

## 👁️ 2. Lower Light — Sense Perception

Enables us to discern natural forms. Begins with a material object and requires corporeal (bodily) light.

**Five divisions** corresponding to the five senses:

10. Sight
11. Hearing
12. Smell
13. Taste
14. Touch

**Discussion:** How does Bonaventure’s account of sense perception relate to Descartes’s worry that the senses deceive us? What about Chalmers’s claim that virtual perception is genuine perception?

---

## 🧠 3. Inner Light — Reason (Philosophical Knowledge)

Reason operates in three domains:

- **Speech** — rational language
- **Natural philosophy** — understanding things
- **Moral philosophy** — understanding conduct

**Six purposes of reason:**

15. Express
16. Teach
17. Persuade
18. Apprehend
19. Judge
20. Motivate

---

## ✨ 4. Higher Light — Grace and Sacred Scripture

The highest form of illumination operates in three domains:

21. **Spiritual truths** — faith
22. **Moral truths** — morals
23. **The purpose of the previous two** — the end toward which faith and morals point

---

## 🔗 Connection to Course Themes

| Bonaventure’s Level | Course Connection |
| --- | --- |
| External / Sense | Chalmers’s virtual perception — is virtual sense experience a genuine lower light? |
| Inner / Reason | The philosophical arguments we’ve been analyzing — Bostrom, Chalmers, Descartes |
| Higher | Does the simulation hypothesis affect the status of higher knowledge claims? |

**Discussion:** If we are in a simulation, which of Bonaventure’s four lights are still available to us? Which might be compromised?

## 📝 Instructor Notes

> [!note] 👨‍🏫
> Bonaventure’s four-lights framework is a useful medieval counterpoint to the modern epistemology we’ve been doing. It shows that the question “what are the sources of knowledge?” is not new. The hierarchy (external → sense → reason → higher) also mirrors the structure of the course: we began with sense experience and simulation, moved to rational argument (Bostrom, Chalmers), and are now approaching value and ethics. The discussion question about which lights survive a simulation is a good bridge to the value section of the course.
