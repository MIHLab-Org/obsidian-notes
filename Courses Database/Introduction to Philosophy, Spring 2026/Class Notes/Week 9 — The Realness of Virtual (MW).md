## 📖 What is Reality?

### Three Definitions of Reality

1. **Everything that exists** — the entire cosmos
2. **A world or worlds** — multiverse, virtual spaces, physical or virtual
3. **A property** — rigidity, the property of being real

### Five Criteria for What’s Real

| Criterion | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Existence** | “What is it to exist?” — a deep question without a definitive answer |
| **Causal Power** | The Eleatic dictum: to be real is to have causal powers |
| **Mind-Independence** | Philip K. Dick’s dictum: “Reality is that which, when you stop believing in it, doesn’t go away.” |
| **Non-illusoriness** | Things are as they seem to be |
| **Genuineness** | Things are authentic, not counterfeit |

> *“I suggest that everything which possesses any power of any kind, either to produce a change in anything of any nature or to be affected even in the least degree by the slightest cause, though it be only on one occasion, has real being.”* — Plato, *Sophist* (Eleatic Stranger)

## 🧠 Key Positions on Virtual Reality

### Physical Realism

- Physical things are the only things that are real
- Virtual things are therefore not real

### Virtual Realism (Chalmers)

- Physical things are *physically* real
- Virtual things are *virtually* real
- Virtual reality is genuine reality — virtual objects are real, not illusions

### Simulation Realism

- If we are in a simulation, the things around us are still real

### Virtual Digitalism

- Objects in virtual reality are digital (not physical) objects

## ⚖️ The No-Illusion View

### Skeptical Scenarios Compared

| Scenario | Author | The Claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evil Demon | Descartes | An evil demon deceives us about all our perceptions |
| Brain-in-a-Vat | Hilary Putnam | A machine feeds us the content of all our perceptions |
| Simulation | Chalmers | We live in a computer-generated world |

### Chalmers’s Response — The No-Illusion View

- In a skeptical scenario, the objects of our perceptions are **not** illusions
- Their *underlying nature* may differ from what we thought
- A flower perceived in a simulation **really is a flower** — its nature is digital, not physical, but it is real

### Discussion Questions

- What might Berkeley’s response be to the evil demon scenario?
- What makes something an illusion? What about illusions that cannot be discovered?
- How does Bouwsma respond to Descartes?

## 💻 What is Virtual Reality? — Chalmers’s Definition

A virtual reality environment is **immersive, interactive, and computer-generated**.

| Property | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Interactive** | Two-way interaction between users and environment, and among objects |
| **Computer-Generated** | A computer generates signals sent to our sensory systems |
| **Immersive** | We experience the environment as a world surrounding us |

### Types of Immersion

- **Psychological immersion** — occupying all attention, flow state
- **Perceptual immersion** — the world as three-dimensional and surrounding
- **Audiovisual immersion** — looks and sounds real
- **Bodily immersion** — physical sense of inhabiting the environment
- **Full immersion** — all senses engaged, no trace of ordinary physical environment

### Susanne Langer on Virtual Worlds

Virtual worlds consist of: objects, space, powers, memory

## 📚 Historical Parallels

## 📝 Instructor Notes

> [!note] 👨‍🏫
> Descartes’s evil-demon and Putnam’s brain-in-a-vat are skeptical views about our ability to know facts about our world. Chalmers’s no-illusion view responds that in a perfect simulation, the things inside it are perfectly real — not because appearance is all there is (Berkeley), but because the objects have the right causal and structural properties. A flower in a simulation really is a flower; its nature is digital rather than physical, but that doesn’t make it unreal. This parallels Berkeley’s response in important ways but is grounded differently.
