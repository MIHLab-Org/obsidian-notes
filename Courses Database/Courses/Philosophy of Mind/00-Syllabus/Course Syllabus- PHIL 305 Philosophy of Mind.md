## Course Overview

This course introduces advanced philosophical thinking about the mind. We use Berit Brogaard's *On Romantic Love* as our primary text, examining foundational questions: What is the mind? How does it relate to the body? What is consciousness? How do mental states represent the world? Who else has a mind? How do we know ourselves?

We approach these questions by focusing on a morally relatable one, the experience of love. Monday sessions work through *On Romantic Love* chapter by chapter; Wednesday sessions take up supporting literature from Tim Bayne, Eleonore Stump, Martha Nussbaum, and Jesse Prinz.

## Learning Outcomes

  - Identify and articulate core problems in philosophy of mind (mind–body problem, consciousness, mental content, other minds)
  - Recognize thought experiments as tools for testing philosophical theories and evaluate their effectiveness
  - Apply philosophical theories (physicalism, functionalism, externalism, etc.) to novel mental phenomena
  - Design and create Socratic dialogues using AI philosopher personas that model rigorous objections
  - Design thought experiments that illuminate problems in philosophy of mind
  - Reflect critically on your own mental phenomena through philosophical analysis
  - Collaborate effectively in philosophical discussions and theory comparison
  - Articulate philosophical positions clearly in writing and discussion
