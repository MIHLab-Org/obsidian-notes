A. Explain, analyze (identifying premises/conclusions), and evaluate (as valid and sound) the argument in a given stretch of text—and to do so in a way that invites reflection on their own reasoned beliefs and commitments.

B. Reflect on you own values, commitments, and *lived *practices in light of course material and class dialogue—including materials and thinkers representing the liberal tradition.

 (i) analyze the argument it contains and 

(ii) respond to and evaluate the argument in a way that requires both 
    (a) comparison to other viewpoints considered in the course (including those representing the Catholic tradition) and 
    (b) reflections on the implications of the argument’s conclusion for the student’s own outlook and way of life. 

1. Choose from one of the three prompts.
	1. You will be given 3 prompts to choose from.
	2. Choose one of these.
2. Explain the Philosophical Debate
	1. This includes articulating the main positions.
	2. Stating what the primary disagreement is between the 3 positions
	3. You might address these questions
		1. What does your main question mean?
		2. Why is it difficult to answer?
		3. What are two different ways someone might answer it?
		4. What reasons might someone give for each answer?
		5. What would count as evidence for or against each position?
3. Pick one of the main positions to write on. "Given materialism, what is a person's true self?"
	1. Turn Your Question into a Testable Hypothesis
		1. Identify what are we investigating?
		2. What would one predict, what might be the default position? Is the true self immaterial? Material?
4. Design Your *Conceptual* Experiment. How would you determine whether you are right or not?
	1. Your experiment should allow you to compare what happens under different conditions.
5. Specify what your evidence would be.
6. Consider an objection to your method.
7. Write your conclusion.
