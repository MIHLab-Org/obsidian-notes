1 Beth is fortunate. So is Carl. Therefore, both Carl and Beth are fortunate.
    Fb, Fc, $\vdash$Fb ^ Fc


2 Theft is not praiseworthy, but blameworthy. Hence theft is blameworthy.
    ~Pt ^ Bt $\vdash$Bt


3 Healing is good. So something is good.
    Hg $\vdash \exists x Gx$


4 Healing is a good act. Therefore some acts are good.
    Ah $\land$ Gh $\vdash \exists x(Ax \land Gx)$


5 Everything is good. Consequently, theft is good.
    $\forall xGx \vdash Gt$


6 Theft is not good. Therefore not everything is good.
    $\neg Gt \vdash \neg \forall xGx$
    $\exist x \neg Gx$


7 Everything good is praiseworthy. Healing is good. Therefore, healing is praiseworthy.
    $\forall x(Gx \rightarrow Px), Gh, \vdash Ph$


8 All just things are praiseworthy. All praiseworthy things are good. Therefore any just
thing is good.
    $\forall x((Jx \rightarrow Px), \forall x(Px \rightarrow Gx) \vdash \forall x(Jx \rightarrow Gx)$


9 Healing is a praiseworthy act. Nothing praiseworthy is blameworthy. Therefore,
healing is not blameworthy.
    Ph $\land$ Ah, $\neg \exists x(Px \land Bx) \vdash \neg Bh$


10 Healing is good, but theft is not. Therefore some things are good and some things
aren't.
    Gh $\land$ ~Gt $\vdash \exists x \exists y (Gx \land \neg Gy)$
