## Episode 1: Could We Be Living in a Simulation?

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)

`[Wake up in a hab bed. Hold on the ceiling, then stand and look out the window.]`

I just woke up in a bed I’ve never physically slept in, inside a city that doesn’t actually exist. Everything here behaves the way a real world is supposed to. So here’s the scary part: how do I know the world I wake up in tomorrow morning is any different?

### 1. Three Old Stories (0:15–2:30)

`[Slow walk through the hab corridor and out to the transit platform.]`

People were asking this question thousands of years before computers or VR headsets even existed. Three classic stories sum up the whole problem:

- **Zhuangzi’s Butterfly:** An ancient Chinese philosopher dreams he’s a butterfly, fluttering around without a care. Then he wakes up—and can’t tell if he’s a man who just dreamed he was a butterfly, or a butterfly right now dreaming he’s a man.
    
- **Narada’s Dream:** In a classic Hindu story, a sage named Narada lives through an entire lifetime—family, kids, tragedy, old age—only to snap back and realize it all happened in a single moment.
    
- **Plato’s Cave:** Prisoners are chained inside a cave their whole lives, staring at shadows on a wall and thinking those shadows are the entire universe.
    

> **The Three Questions**
> 
> - How can Zhuangzi actually _know_ whether or not he’s dreaming?
>     
> - Was Narada’s other life real, or just an illusion?
>     
> - And if we’re stuck in Plato’s cave, can we still live a good life?
>     

### 2. What a Simulation Actually Is (2:30–4:30)

`[Board a ship. Show the cockpit instruments, then take off.]`

At its core, a simulation is just a rough approximation of how a system behaves. Think about how games like _Elite Dangerous_ or _Star Citizen_ work. There are four levels to how a simulation relates to reality:

1. **Representation:** Showing the world as it actually is right now. `[Shot: a realistic city skyline.]`
    
2. **Could:** Showing how the world _could_ be in the future. `[Shot: a quantum jump.]`
    
3. **Could Have:** Showing how the world _could have_ been if things went differently. `[Shot: a crashed derelict.]`
    
4. **Simulacrum:** Pushing past reality altogether into pure possible worlds—using the sim in ways that bear zero resemblance to real life. `[Shot: a pirate base.]`
    

### 3. Two Kinds of Sims (4:30–6:30)

`[Quantum travel across the system.]`

Not all simulated beings are the same. There’s a huge difference between a **biosim** and a **pure sim**:

- A **biosim** (or impure sim) is a biological person plugged in from the outside—like me controlling my character right now.
    
- A **pure sim** lives 100% inside the simulation, with no body outside—like the NPC selling me fuel.
    

There’s also scale: a **local** simulation runs one slice of a world, like _Star Citizen_. A **global** simulation runs the entire universe. _Star Citizen_ is a local sim packed with biosims. The **Simulation Hypothesis** asks: what if our universe is a global simulation—and we’re all pure sims?

> **The Simulation Hypothesis:** We are living in a simulation—we are, and have always been, inside a computer simulation of the world.

### 4. Can You Prove It Either Way? (6:30–7:45)

`[Stop at a viewpoint and look at the planet.]`

Here’s the catch: we can’t prove we’re in a simulation—but we can’t prove we’re _not_ in one, either. And that’s the puzzle this whole series is going to unpack: if you can’t prove it either way, what does that mean for what you can know, what counts as real, and how you should live your life?
