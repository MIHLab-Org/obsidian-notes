Your group project will be comprised of 3 parts, 2 persons each, and each group will be comprised of 6 students. If groups cannot be split evenly into groups of 6, then one group will receive special instructions for its project. Each student will be graded on one part of the project, making each group member’s grade (potentially) different. Students will separate into groups by the second week of the semester, `r advdate(mon, 2)`, and decide which student will be responsible for which portion of the project. I will provide an opportunity for each each class member to get to know one another and select your groups. If this proves difficult, please contact me and we will figure out a solution. 

The 3 parts of the project are as follows: 

Game narrative development (to be explained in the assignment supplemental document), 

Presentation (this will be scene development, your job will be to develop the setting in which the narrative takes place), and research (you will provide an analysis of the project, this is the most philosophically rigorous portion of the project. You will need to tie up any loose ends, providing the reader a bird's eye view of the philosophical issue being addressed). Students can and should help one another for each portion, but only 2 students will be responsible (i.e., graded) for each part. Please see me if experiencing any concerns with your portion of the assignment well before the due date at the end of the semester.

Topics for the project must be agreed upon by the members and approved by the instructor before any research can begin. Project topics will be largely up to the group members, with a few constraints. Topics must be sufficiently narrow to ensure project completion and project quality. Topics should focus on one of the areas of philosophy discussed in this course—philosophical questions, ethical virtues, philosophical theories, or dilemmas. For more information on this project, see the Group Project Assignment document.
