## Course Participation & Attendance

**Attendance**: Expected at all classes. Missing more than 3 classes (without documented excuse) may impact your grade.

**Participation**: Philosophy thrives on dialogue. You are expected to:

- Engage thoughtfully in class discussions
- Ask questions when confused
- Respect others' views while challenging ideas
- Contribute to group projects
- Participate in game sessions

**In-class exercises**: Throughout the semester, we'll do brief philosophical exercises (identifying arguments, evaluating thought experiments, etc.). These aren't formally graded but demonstrate your engagement.

## Course Policies

### Technology

- **Laptops/tablets**: Encouraged for accessing readings and taking notes
- **Phones**: Should be silent and put away during class
- **AI tools**: You may use Claude to help brainstorm game events or refine writing, but the thinking and final work must be yours. Cite any AI assistance.

### Academic Integrity

All work must be your own. Plagiarism (copying from readings or other students without attribution) will result in a failing grade on that assignment. See Stetson's honor code.

### Accessibility

If you require accommodations (extra time, note-taking assistance, etc.), contact the Accessibility Services Office and notify me.

### Late Work

- **Weeklies**: Accepted up to 1 week late with 10% penalty
- **Game Session reflections**: Accepted up to 1 week late with 10% penalty
- **Persona projects**: Accepted up to 2 days late with 10% penalty; after that, contact me
- **Final Project**: No late submissions accepted (hard deadline 12/11)

## Philosophy as Moral Practice

Throughout this course, we approach philosophy as more than intellectual exercise. Philosophy is a **moral practice**—a discipline that cultivates virtue by:

- **Intellectual humility**: Recognizing what we don't know
- **Rigor**: Following arguments wherever they lead
- **Openness**: Genuinely considering views that challenge our own
- **Wonder**: Maintaining awe at life's deep questions
- **Community**: Building the "union of love appropriate to friendship" through shared inquiry

When we engage with thought experiments, we're not just testing logical consistency. We're training ourselves to think deeply, to imagine alternatives, and to recognize that ideas have real consequences for how we live.

This is what makes philosophy transformative.

**I'm struggling with the material**: Email me or come to office hours. Philosophy is hard; that's the point. We'll work through it together.

## Final Thought

Philosophy begins with wonder. It's the practice of standing still, suspending what we think we know, and asking: "But what if I'm wrong? What does this really mean? Why does it matter?"

In this course, we do that together—through reading, discussion, game-based thought experiments, and dialogue with AI philosophers who ask better questions than we ask ourselves.

Welcome to philosophy. Let's think deeply about what matters.
