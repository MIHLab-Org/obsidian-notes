## Course Overview

This course introduces advanced philosophical thinking about the mind. We use a comprehensive exploration of contemporary philosophy of mind as our primary text, examining foundational questions: What is the mind? How does it relate to the body? What is consciousness? How do mental states represent the world? Who else has a mind? How do we know ourselves?

We approach these questions through rigorous conceptual analysis, engaging with physicalism, externalism, theories of consciousness, and the metaphysics of mental causation. The course bridges classical problems (the mind-body problem, other minds, self-knowledge) with contemporary research in philosophy of mind.

**Core Thesis:** Understanding the mind requires integrating multiple perspectives—from physical science to subjective experience, from mental content to consciousness, from individual minds to minds in a shared world. Philosophy of mind cultivates the intellectual virtue of conceptual precision: the ability to define terms rigorously, identify hidden assumptions, and reason carefully through competing theories.

**Pedagogical Approach:** You will engage with philosophy of mind by: reading and analyzing foundational texts carefully • designing thought experiments that test philosophical claims • creating AI philosopher personas that model sophisticated objections • collaborating with peers to evaluate competing theories • reflecting critically on your own intuitions about mental phenomena. You don’t just understand the hard problem abstractly; you work through concrete cases. You don’t just read about intentionality; you explore how mental content grounds meaning.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By course end, you will be able to:
- Identify and articulate core problems in philosophy of mind (mind-body problem, consciousness, mental content, other minds)
- Recognize thought experiments as tools for testing philosophical theories and evaluate their effectiveness
- Apply philosophical theories (physicalism, functionalism, externalism, etc.) to novel mental phenomena
- Design and create Socratic dialogues using AI philosopher personas that model rigorous objections
- Design thought experiments that illuminate problems in philosophy of mind
- Reflect critically on your own mental phenomena (perception, thought, consciousness) through philosophical analysis
- Collaborate effectively in philosophical discussions and theory comparison
- Articulate philosophical positions clearly in writing and discussion, with careful attention to conceptual precision

## Final Thought

Philosophy of mind asks: What is it like to have a mind? How can we understand the most intimate aspect of our existence through reason? In this course, we pursue conceptual clarity about consciousness, intentionality, and selfhood—not as abstract exercises, but as rigorous attempts to make sense of what we are. We read carefully, think deeply, and challenge our assumptions together. Welcome to philosophy of mind. Let’s understand the mind.
