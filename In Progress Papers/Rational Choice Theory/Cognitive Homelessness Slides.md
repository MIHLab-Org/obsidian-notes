A cognitive home is a domain of "recognizable and unmistakable facts". These facts included ones about our own sense data. It was once believed that our beliefs where sensitive to this domain. However, we can make mistakes with respect to our own experiences, including what those experiences seem to be to us. This outcome provides evidence that there is no such home (see Jeffrey, 1983; Williamson, 2000).

---

For agents like us, susceptible to human types of ignorance and error, the advice to maximize expected value will not be good, fully general advice for decision-making.
	Maximize Expected Value advises us to act according to what we take the world to be like
	However, trying to follow its recommendations can leave us predictably worse off
		Sometimes, we are not certain what we take the world to be like, either.
		And when we are not certain what we take the world to be like, we may fail to do what it tells us to do.

---

Here I focus on those times where we are not certain what we take the world to be like. I argue that there maybe methods to increase our credence in a small set of cases.

---

## MEV

Expected Utility
	A rational agent following expected utility and doing whatever has the most expected value for that agent, has both a credence function and a value function as defined over a set of possible worlds (Lewis, David. n.d. _Causal Decision Theory_.).

---

I.e., there is some world $w$ such that:

Some Value Function $V(W)$: 
	 - i.e., [be] “your own boss”

and

Some Credence Function $C(W)$:
	- i.e, “Stacked your bread”
    - bought your own Mercedes

Is true at $w$

---

Consider the following oddity:
> It is easier to be confident you are in love, when you are in it than to be confident you are out of love, when you are out of it. The epistemological significance of cases with this structure (or similar) was originally observed in Lasonen-Aarnio (2015), and has been discussed in Salow (2018) and Isaacs and Russell (2023).
