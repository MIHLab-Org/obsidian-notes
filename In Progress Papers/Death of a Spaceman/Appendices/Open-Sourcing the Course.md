## Public-Domain Substitutes

Most copyrighted readings have a close public-domain ancestor, often the very text the modern author is answering. About five have no real equivalent. Those are marked **None** and should stay as links or e-reserves. _Reality+_ stays a purchased text either way.

**Rule of thumb (US):** works published before 1931 are public domain as of 2026. A translation must be public domain too: use Jowett (Plato), Ross 1908/1925 (Aristotle), Haldane & Ross 1911 (Descartes), Müller or Meiklejohn (Kant), Bernard 1892 (Kant's third _Critique_), Dominican Fathers 1920 (Aquinas), Beveridge 1845 (Calvin).

## Where to Get Each Reading

Every public-domain reading in the core chapters has a free, legal online copy. The links below are the cleanest copy found for each, checked November 2026. The Project Gutenberg, MIT Classics and Fordham editions are searchable text; the Internet Archive and Wellcome items are page scans, so use them when you need original pagination. Copyrighted readings are listed with an author-hosted copy where one exists; otherwise they go through library e-reserve.

**A good free (but not public-domain) option for students:** Jonathan Bennett's [Early Modern Texts](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com) puts Descartes, Reid, Berkeley, Hume and others into plain modern English. Its PDFs are free to read and link, but Bennett holds the copyright, so link them rather than reprinting them.
