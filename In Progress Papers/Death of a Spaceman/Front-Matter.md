**Editor:** Montaque Reynolds, PhD — Stetson University / MIHLab

**Preface** [800–1,200 words]

- Why games: a game is a possible world you can actually enter, which turns a thought experiment into something you can run.
- Why these two games: _Elite Dangerous_ (procedural galaxy, ship systems, permadeath-adjacent stakes) and _Star Citizen_ (dense human settings, in-world art objects, Stanton system locations).
- What the book is not: a game guide, or an argument that games replace texts.

**Note to instructors** [500 words]

- How the labs map onto a semester (see the schedule at the end).
- Access and equity: free _Elite Dangerous_ base game; _Star Citizen_ free-fly weeks or instructor stream as fallback; a non-player alternative for every lab (watch a recorded run, then write).
- Assessment: the argument rubric and lab report template in the Appendices.

**Note to students: how to run a philosophical experiment** [600 words]

Adapt the four-step structure from your syllabus:

1. The claim (from Chalmers or another philosopher).
2. Your intuition about that claim.
3. The experiment you ran in the game to test it.
4. Your finding: did your position change, and why?

**Note to students: how to read an argument** [600 words] — claim, premises, conclusion; valid vs. sound; one worked example (the master argument for skepticism).
