---
title: Letter of Recommendation for Anne-Marie
---
To Whom it May Concern,

I am a visiting assistant professor of philosophy at Stetson University. I defended my dissertation on moral epistemology at Saint Louis University in 2024. My interests are generally in the areas of mind, epistemology, and aesthetics. I have known Anne-Marie since the first class I taught at Stetson University, when she was a student in my Introduction to Philosophy course.

The needs of the student body here at Stetson are unique. Because of pressing financial responsibilities, our students are most often motivated by financial constraints. They generally view graduation and decisions about courses of study through the lens of economic security, though not always immediate security. For this reason, our pre-law offerings are often very attractive to our student recruits. Anne-Marie's philosophical interests, however, are genuinely intrinsic. She is interested in understanding the world around her, asking deep questions, and challenging assumptions that may seem obvious to others. She has always been courteous. I never got the feeling that she was behind on the reading. On the rare occasions she was, she was very apologetic, and it was never without good reason: the only times she was unprepared were when she had gone down a rabbit hole on a related reading. This usually meant that she was more than prepared to discuss a specific point related to the reading, generally something she wished the author had spent more time on.

On many occasions she seemed to be the only one prepared to discuss the material, or she was far more prepared than the rest of the class. She always asked the best questions, and she did a great job of recognizing the main point of an argument and applying it in a way that helped the rest of the class better understand the subject matter. Although it can be difficult here at Stetson to motivate the kinds of philosophical concerns that have been raised in related questions more generally, Anne-Marie always gave these issues the same care and attention she gave to those more closely aligned with her own philosophical aims and pursuits. Her favorite interests have revolved around questions rooted in ontology, especially in the philosophy of religion. She is careful to focus on and immerse herself in the debates we cover, and she always applies herself to better understanding the views of authors who generally had the freedom to engage in such debates, given that many of these debates may seem frivolous to the general reader. She was always able to appreciate the content of an argument, rather than only its form, which I have often found refreshing.

It is my opinion that an environment that encourages deep and critical reflection, focus, and contemplation is the kind of environment in which Anne-Marie would excel. I think that Anne-Marie would make an excellent addition to your program and I enthusiastically recommend her.
