Davidson, Donald. 2008. “A Coherence Theory of Truth and Knowledge.” In _Epistemology: An Anthology_, 2nd ed, edited by Ernest Sosa. Blackwell Philosophy Anthologies 11. Blackwell Pub.

We can accept objective truth conditions as the key to meaning, a realist view of truth, and we can insist that knowledge is of an objective world independent of our thought or language.

However,

> But from each person's own vantage point, there must be a graded presumption in favor of each of his own beliefs. (Davidson 2008, 132)

1. There is a presumption in favor of the truth of a belief that coheres with a significant mass of belief.
	1. A person's belief that *p* is true because when he attempts to falsify it, he does so in contrast to the other beliefs that he holds.
	2. These other beliefs are taken as true by that believer.
		1. "First I urge that a correct understanding of the speech, beliefs, desires, intentions and other propositional attitudes of a person leads to the conclusion that most of a person's beliefs must be true, and so there is a legitimate presumption that any one of them, if it coheres with most of the rest, is true" (p. 129).
2. Every belief in a coherent total set of beliefs is justified in the light of this presumption, much as every intentional action taken by a rational agent . . . is justified (p. 123).
3. Therefore:
	1. Truth is correspondence with the way things are.
	2. A theory of knowledge that allows that we can know the truth must be a non-relativized, non-internal form of realism.
