Author argues that imaginative resistance occurs when epistemic norms of consistency come into conflict with an author's prescriptions. Brian Weatherson gives an example recalling a famous highway scene. 

A couple is seen fighting on the side of a busy freeway causing traffic to pile up. A passing motorist shoots them dead. The audience is instructed to accept this form of violence as justified, but instead, we are supposed to be repulsed. 

However, if we consider other examples such as Pulp Fiction, Grand Theft Auto, Fargo among others, the violence is absurd and that's the point. This seems to be one of those instances. It may just be that the resistance depends on the choice of the audience. 

They may choose to be repulsed, or choose to imagine that the shooting is justified. If so, then there isn't a similar imaginative resistance because the absurdity is the point.

There are first two possible reasons for this:

 1. Inability to imagine impossibilities
 2. Unwillingness to imagine impossibilities

We might take the one over the other.

A key qualifier of this claim is that the divergence occurs when we realize that we've mistaken some concept employed by the author of a fiction. As such, the imaginative resistance comes from an inability to imagine impossibilities. Perhaps this is similar to trying to imagine a chilliongon. After the 100th side, we just give up.

Accordingly then, there is some fact that we are to imagine but are unable to.
