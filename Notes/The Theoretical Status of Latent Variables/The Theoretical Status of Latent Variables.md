The Principle of Local Independence:
> The basic statistical idea of latent variables analysis is simple. If a latent variable underlies a number of observed variables, then conditionalizing on that latent variable will render the observed variables statistically independent. (p. 203)

However, the theoretical status of latent variables has not been analyzed in models of psychological measurement.

1. Should we assume that the latent variable signifies a real entity or conceive of it as a useful fiction, constructed by the human mind?
2. Should we say that we measure a latent variable in the sense that it underlies and determines our observations, or is it more appropriately considered to be constructed out of the observed scores?
3. What exactly constitutes the relation between latent variables and observed scores?
4. Is this relation of a causal nature? 
5. If so, in what sense? 
6. Is latent variable theory neutral with respect to these issues?
# Three Ways of Looking at the Latent Variable

The latent variable generally appears both as a formal-theoretical concept and as an operational-empirical concept. But these two concepts need to be connected by a third concept.
	Formal: i.e., mathematical
	Empirical: Function of the observed scores (weighted sumscore).
However, the formal and empirical concepts need to be connected. The literature naturally does this:
	by conceiving of such a weighted sumscore as an “estimate” of or as a “proxy” for the latent variable of interest.
		For instance: the weighted sumscore of all items loading on the factor extraversion would be considered an estimate of the level of extraversion.

There are three possibilities for the third concept:
1. Realism
2. Constructivism
3. Operationalism

It is argued that realism is the third connecting concept because

> The realist view requires an account of the relation between the latent variable and its indicators, for which causality is a natural candidate.
## The Formal Stance: Syntax

Formal: i.e., mathematical

Modern Test Theory Models
	Confirmatory Factory Models
	IRT Models
		the relation between the latent variable and the observed scores is mathematically explicit
	GLIRT Models
		In GLIRT, the relation between the latent variable and the observed scores is a generalized regression function (mathematical explication) of the observed scores on the latent variable.
			This regression function may differ in form (e.g., it is linear for the factor model but logistic for the Rasch, 1960, model; see also Mellenbergh, 1994). For instance, in a factor model for general intelligence, one would specify that an increase of n units in the latent variable leads to an increase of n times the factor loading in the expected value of a given item.

There are two things one can do on the basis of the set of formal assumptions underlying latent variables analysis.
1. Determine how observed scores would behave if they were “generated” under our model (both mathematical derivations and simulation studies).
2. We can also develop plausible procedures to estimate parameters in the model on the basis of manifest scores, given the assumption that these scores were generated by our model.

However, formal derivations are tautologous and so do not reveal anything about the world external to the syntactical system.
