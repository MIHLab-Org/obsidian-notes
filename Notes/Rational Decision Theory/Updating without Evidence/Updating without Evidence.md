# In Summary

Planning to follow an updating rule does not generally guarantee that you will successfully conform to it, but there is some connection between your planned rule and your future credences.

### The Problem

1. We do not have beliefs but rather we have credences, degrees of belief between one and zero.
2. Beliefs should maximize epistemic utility.
		1. If a proposition is true, then the credence with highest epistemic utility is 1 for that proposition is 1.
		2. If a proposition is false, then the credence with the highest epistemic utility for that proposition is 0.
		3. Therefore our credences should follow the truth rule: assign one to every truth, and zero to every falsehood.
		4. If your credences should follow the truth rule, than our credences should pursue accuracy and we should aim to believe the truth.
			1. However, our attitudes are not sensitive enough to follow the truth rule.
			2. If we achieve truth, it would just be by luck. (compare Horowitz, 2019; Holguín, forthcoming; Dorst & Mandelkern, forthcoming)
			3. Therefore we should plan to follow the truth rule.
		5.  We should plan to follow the truth rule.
## Deterministic Guess Model

An agent’s credences are determined by their guess, which was deterministically settled by the state of the world.
### The Truth Rule and the Nap

> My daughter is at the age where she’s dropping her nap. She takes an afternoon nap roughly half the time. On the days when she has a good nap, she is usually in a good mood in the evening. On the days when she doesn’t have a good nap, she is often really cranky. What I am wondering is what degree of belief I should have that she will be cranky this evening, _after_ I find out whether [or not] she naps.

Truth rule: plan to be _sure_ that she will be cranky if she really will be cranky, or else be _sure_ she won’t be cranky if she really won’t be.

* if daughter naps, then in Nap-and-not-Cranky state, she will not be cranky, so place all credence in that state
* if no nap, then no-Nap-and-not-Cranky state , she will be cranky, and place all credence in this state
* so either in nap or no-nap state
* truth rule would be inaccurate
## Generalized Deterministic Guess Model 1

Given these assumptions about my powers of discrimination, the set of three states E(n) = {n − 1, n, n + 1} really specifies something I ought to plan to proportion my belief to.

The set E(n) includes the states that are “close” to n, in the sense that they are states I could easily mistake for n, if I guess which state I’m in.
## Generalized Deterministic Guess Model 2

The rule that is best for me to plan to follow sometimes prescribes changing my credences without becoming certain in anything new.

* You assign case 1 prior credence 1∕2 and cases 2 and 3 prior credence 1∕4 each. 
* If you plan to follow a certain rule f, then you will end up having the credences f(Friend) in cases 1 and 2, and the credences f(Stranger) in case 3. 
* There are a few fiddly details here about scoring rules (see Appendix C) but things work out basically the way you would expect: 
	* since case 1, where you see your friend, is twice as probable as case 2, where you don’t, 
	* the optimal credence function to have for those cases gives twice as much probability to seeing your friend as to seeing a stranger. 
* Meanwhile, in case 3 it’s best to be sure it’s a stranger, since that’s the only way case 3 can arise.
